# Lesson 4: Resolution Strategies with Formal Implications

## 1. First-Principles Intuition & Invariants

Every logical paradox is, at its core, a stress test against the implicit contracts of a formal system. When the Liar sentence announces *"This statement is false,"* it does not merely produce a curiosity; it exposes a structural crack in any system that permits unrestricted self-reference over a truth predicate that ranges across its own language. The resolution strategies that logicians developed over the twentieth century are not patches slapped over syntactic wounds. They are deep architectural decisions about the ontology of truth, the topology of semantic evaluation, and the acceptable expressiveness of formal languages. Understanding them as a practitioner means internalizing not just what each strategy forbids, but *why* the prohibition is precisely shaped the way it is, and what computational and proof-theoretic consequences follow inevitably from each choice.
