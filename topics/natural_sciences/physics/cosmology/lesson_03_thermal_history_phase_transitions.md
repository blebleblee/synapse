# Lesson 3: Thermal History through Phase Transitions

## 1. First-Principles Intuition & Invariants

The universe began not as a static structure but as a dynamically evolving thermal system governed by the interplay of quantum field theory, general relativity, and statistical mechanics. Understanding cosmology requires abandoning intuitive notions of "before" and "after" in favor of a framework where spacetime itself emerges from quantum fluctuations, where particle species blink in and out of thermal equilibrium, and where symmetries spontaneously break as the cosmic temperature plummets from $10^{32}$ Kelvin to the present-day microwave background at 2.7 Kelvin. This lesson dissects the thermal history of the universe through its critical phase transitions, treating the cosmos as a laboratory where particle physics experiments unfold at energy scales inaccessible to terrestrial accelerators.

The foundational principle governing early universe thermodynamics is **adiabatic expansion under Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric dynamics**. As spacetime expands according to the scale factor $a(t)$, photon wavelengths stretch as $\lambda \propto a(t)$, and the cosmic temperature scales inversely with the scale factor: $T(t) \propto a(t)^{-1}$. This redshifting mechanism drives the universe through a sequence of phase transitions where particles fall out of thermal equilibrium, symmetries break, and the fundamental forces we observe today crystallize from a unified high-energy state. The entropy per comoving volume remains nearly constant during adiabatic expansion, expressed through the conservation relation $s \, a^3 = \text{constant}$, where $s$ is the entropy density. This entropy conservation becomes the scaffolding upon which we reconstruct the entire thermal history.

At the Planck epoch ($t \sim 10^{-43}$ seconds, $T \sim 10^{32}$ K, $E \sim 10^{19}$ GeV), quantum gravitational effects dominate and spacetime itself becomes a probabilistic foam of fluctuating geometries. No complete theory of quantum gravity exists, but inflationary cosmology posits that a scalar field (the inflaton) drove exponential expansion, stretching quantum fluctuations to cosmological scales and setting initial conditions for structure formation. As inflation ended through reheating, the inflaton decayed into Standard Model particles, establishing thermal equilibrium in a radiation-dominated universe.

The electroweak phase transition occurred around $T \sim 100$ GeV ($t \sim 10^{-11}$ seconds), where the Higgs field acquired a non-zero vacuum expectation value, breaking the unified electroweak symmetry $SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ into the electromagnetic $U(1)_\text{EM}$ and leaving the weak force short-ranged through $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ boson mass generation. This spontaneous symmetry breaking fundamentally altered the particle mass spectrum, giving fermions their observed masses through Yukawa couplings to the Higgs field. The phase transition may have been first-order (with nucleation bubbles and latent heat release) or a smooth crossover, depending on Higgs mass and top quark mass—parameters constrained by LHC measurements to suggest a crossover transition.

The quark-hadron phase transition at $T \sim 150$ MeV ($t \sim 10^{-5}$ seconds) marked the confinement of quarks and gluons into color-neutral hadrons. Above this critical temperature, quarks and gluons existed in a quark-gluon plasma (QGP) with asymptotic freedom allowing weakly interacting behavior at high energies. As the temperature dropped below the QCD scale $\Lambda_\text{QCD} \sim 200$ MeV, the strong force coupling constant grew large, and chiral symmetry breaking generated constituent quark masses dynamically. Quarks bound into protons, neutrons, pions, and heavier hadrons, with the baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta \equiv n_B / n_\gamma \sim 6 \times 10^{-10}$ already imprinted by baryogenesis mechanisms operating at higher energies.

Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) transpired between $t \sim 1$ second and $t \sim 200$ seconds, when the temperature dropped from $T \sim 1$ MeV to $T \sim 0.1$ MeV, allowing nuclear fusion to synthesize light elements. The weak interaction freeze-out fixed the neutron-to-proton ratio through the equilibrium reaction $n \leftrightarrow p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$. Once the weak interaction rate $\Gamma_\text{weak} \sim G_F^2 T^5$ fell below the Hubble expansion rate $H \sim \sqrt{g_* \rho / 3M_\text{Pl}^2}$, the neutron fraction froze out at $n/p \sim 1/7$. Nearly all surviving neutrons fused into helium-4 through the reaction chain $p + n \to D + \gamma$, $D + D \to {}^3\text{He} + n$ or ${}^3\text{H} + p$, and ${}^3\text{He} + n \to {}^4\text{He} + \gamma$. The primordial helium-4 mass fraction emerged as $Y_p \sim 0.25$, with trace amounts of deuterium, helium-3, and lithium-7. These predictions match spectroscopic observations of metal-poor gas clouds with exquisite precision, confirming the hot Big Bang thermal history and constraining the effective number of relativistic species $N_\text{eff} \sim 3.0$ (consistent with three neutrino flavors).

Photon decoupling and recombination occurred at $T \sim 0.3$ eV ($z \sim 1100$, $t \sim 380{,}000$ years), when electrons and protons combined into neutral hydrogen through the reaction $p + e^- \to H + \gamma$. The Saha equation governs the ionization fraction $x_e \equiv n_e / (n_e + n_H)$:

$$\frac{x_e^2}{1 - x_e} = \frac{1}{n_B} \left( \frac{m_e T}{2\pi} \right)^{3/2} \exp\left( -\frac{13.6 \, \text{eV}}{T} \right)$$

where $n_B$ is the baryon number density and $13.6$ eV is the hydrogen binding energy. Recombination proceeded as a two-step process: direct recombination to the ground state $p + e^- \to H(1s) + \gamma$ produced ionizing photons that immediately re-ionized hydrogen, creating a bottleneck. Instead, cascade recombination through excited states $p + e^- \to H(n \geq 2) + \gamma$ followed by de-excitation to the ground state dominated. The ionization fraction dropped from $x_e \sim 1$ to $x_e \sim 10^{-3}$ over a redshift interval $\Delta z \sim 100$, rendering the universe transparent to Thomson scattering. Photons streamed freely, creating the cosmic microwave background (CMB) we observe today as a near-perfect blackbody at $T_0 = 2.725$ K. The CMB temperature anisotropies $\Delta T / T \sim 10^{-5}$ encode acoustic oscillations in the photon-baryon fluid before decoupling, providing a snapshot of density perturbations seeded during inflation.

The baryon asymmetry problem—why the universe contains matter rather than equal parts matter and antimatter—requires mechanisms operating at high energies before BBN. Sakharov's three conditions for baryogenesis demand: (1) baryon number violation, (2) C and CP symmetry violation, and (3) departure from thermal equilibrium. Electroweak baryogenesis invokes CP-violating interactions during the electroweak phase transition, while leptogenesis posits that heavy right-handed Majorana neutrinos decay asymmetrically into leptons and antileptons, with the lepton asymmetry later converted to baryon asymmetry through electroweak sphaleron processes. Both scenarios predict the observed ratio $\eta \sim 6 \times 10^{-10}$, though distinguishing between them experimentally remains an open challenge.

The transition from radiation domination to matter domination occurred at $z_\text{eq} \sim 3400$, when the energy density in non-relativistic matter (dark matter plus baryons) equaled the radiation energy density. The Friedmann equation governing the scale factor evolution in a flat universe reads:

$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \rho_\text{total} = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_m a^{-3} + \rho_r a^{-4} + \rho_\Lambda \right)$$

where $\rho_m$, $\rho_r$, and $\rho_\Lambda$ are the matter, radiation, and dark energy densities today. During radiation domination ($a \ll a_\text{eq}$), the scale factor grew as $a(t) \propto t^{1/2}$, while matter domination ($a_\text{eq} \ll a \ll a_\Lambda$) drove $a(t) \propto t^{2/3}$. Dark energy domination began around $z \sim 0.3$ and drives accelerated expansion $\ddot{a} > 0$ today.

Throughout these transitions, **thermal equilibrium** serves as the organizing principle. A particle species remains in thermal equilibrium when its interaction rate $\Gamma$ exceeds the Hubble expansion rate $H$. Once $\Gamma < H$, the species decouples and its phase-space distribution freezes out. Neutrinos decoupled at $T \sim 1$ MeV when the weak interaction rate dropped below $H$, leaving a cosmic neutrino background (C$\nu$B) with temperature $T_\nu = (4/11)^{1/3} T_\gamma \sim 1.95$ K today. Dark matter decoupled when its annihilation rate fell below $H$, with the relic abundance determined by the freeze-out temperature and annihilation cross-section through the Boltzmann equation:

$$\frac{dn}{dt} + 3Hn = -\langle \sigma v \rangle \left( n^2 - n_\text{eq}^2 \right)$$

Solving this equation yields the WIMP miracle: if dark matter has electroweak-scale mass and annihilation cross-section $\langle \sigma v \rangle \sim \alpha_\text{EW}^2 / m_\chi^2$, the relic density naturally matches the observed $\Omega_\text{DM} h^2 \sim 0.12$.

## 2. Core Architecture & Mechanics

The thermal history unfolds as a sequence of epochs, each characterized by the dominant energy density component, temperature scaling law, and active particle species in equilibrium. We now dissect the internal mechanics governing each epoch transition, treating the expanding universe as a coupled system of quantum fields, relativistic fluids, and gravitational dynamics.

### Planck Epoch and Inflationary Reheating

At the Planck time $t_\text{Pl} = \sqrt{\hbar G / c^5} \sim 5.4 \times 10^{-44}$ seconds, the gravitational radius $r_g \sim G E / c^4$ equals the quantum Compton wavelength $\lambda_C \sim \hbar / (E/c)$, rendering spacetime a quantum foam where classical geometry breaks down. The Planck energy $E_\text{Pl} = \sqrt{\hbar c^5 / G} \sim 1.2 \times 10^{19}$ GeV defines the frontier where quantum gravity becomes indispensable. Inflationary models posit a scalar field $\phi$ (the inflaton) with potential energy $V(\phi)$ dominating the energy density:

$$\rho_\phi = \frac{1}{2} \dot{\phi}^2 + V(\phi), \quad p_\phi = \frac{1}{2} \dot{\phi}^2 - V(\phi)$$

When the potential energy dominates ($V(\phi) \gg \dot{\phi}^2 / 2$), the equation of state approaches $p \approx -\rho$, driving accelerated expansion $\ddot{a} / a = H^2 (1 + 3w/2) > 0$ for $w \equiv p/\rho < -1/3$. The inflaton field rolls slowly down the potential, with dynamics governed by the Klein-Gordon equation:

$$\ddot{\phi} + 3H\dot{\phi} + V'(\phi) = 0$$

Quantum fluctuations $\delta\phi$ during inflation become stretched to superhorizon scales, seeding the density perturbations observed in the CMB. The power spectrum of curvature perturbations is:

$$\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) = \frac{1}{24\pi^2 M_\text{Pl}^2} \frac{V}{\epsilon} \quad \text{where} \quad \epsilon \equiv \frac{M_\text{Pl}^2}{2} \left( \frac{V'}{V} \right)^2$$

Inflation ends when $\epsilon \sim 1$, after which the inflaton oscillates coherently around the minimum of $V(\phi)$, decaying into Standard Model particles through parametric resonance (preheating) or perturbative decay (reheating). The reheating temperature $T_\text{rh}$ is constrained by requiring successful BBN ($T_\text{rh} > 4$ MeV to produce light elements) and avoiding overproduction of gravitinos or other relics ($T_\text{rh} < 10^9$ GeV in supersymmetric models).

### Grand Unification and Electroweak Symmetry Breaking

Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) propose that the strong, weak, and electromagnetic forces merge into a single interaction at energy scales $E_\text{GUT} \sim 10^{16}$ GeV. The gauge group $SU(3)_C \times SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ unifies into $SU(5)$, $SO(10)$, or $E_6$ at high energies, with symmetry breaking driven by Higgs fields acquiring vacuum expectation values. Proton decay mediated by GUT-scale gauge bosons ($p \to e^+ + \pi^0$) provides experimental signatures, though current limits $\tau_p > 10^{34}$ years constrain GUT models.

The electroweak phase transition at $T_\text{EW} \sim 100$ GeV occurs when the Higgs field potential:

$$V(\phi, T) = -\mu^2(T) |\phi|^2 + \lambda |\phi|^4 + \text{thermal corrections}$$

develops a non-zero minimum $\langle \phi \rangle = v(T)$. At high temperatures, thermal corrections restore electroweak symmetry ($\langle \phi \rangle = 0$), but as $T$ drops below $T_c$, the symmetry breaks spontaneously. The effective Higgs mass is:

$$m_H^2(T) = m_H^2(T=0) + c T^2$$

where $c \sim g^2 + g'^2 + y_t^2$ includes contributions from gauge couplings and the top Yukawa coupling. The transition is first-order if $v(T_c) / T_c > 1$, producing nucleation bubbles, but LHC measurements ($m_H \sim 125$ GeV, $m_t \sim 173$ GeV) suggest a smooth crossover. Electroweak baryogenesis relies on CP violation during a first-order transition, so the observed smooth crossover disfavors this mechanism unless extended Higgs sectors (e.g., two-Higgs-doublet models) modify the phase structure.

### Quark-Hadron Transition and QCD Thermodynamics

The quark-gluon plasma to hadron gas transition occurs when the temperature drops below the QCD scale $\Lambda_\text{QCD} \sim 200$ MeV. The QCD Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L}_\text{QCD} = -\frac{1}{4} G^a_{\mu\nu} G^{a,\mu\nu} + \sum_f \bar{q}_f (i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m_f) q_f$$

exhibits asymptotic freedom ($\alpha_s \to 0$ as $Q^2 \to \infty$) and infrared slavery ($\alpha_s \to \infty$ as $Q^2 \to 0$), where $G^a_{\mu\nu}$ is the gluon field strength tensor and $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - ig_s A^a_\mu T^a$ is the covariant derivative. Lattice QCD simulations compute the equation of state $p(T, \mu_B)$ as a function of temperature $T$ and baryon chemical potential $\mu_B$, revealing a smooth crossover at $\mu_B = 0$ (early universe conditions) with critical temperature $T_c \sim 155$ MeV.

Chiral symmetry breaking generates constituent quark masses dynamically through quark condensate formation $\langle \bar{q} q \rangle \neq 0$. The light current quark masses ($m_u \sim 2$ MeV, $m_d \sim 5$ MeV) become effective constituent masses $M_q \sim 300$ MeV inside hadrons, with the mass gap arising from non-perturbative QCD dynamics. Pions emerge as pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone bosons of broken chiral symmetry, with masses protected by current quark masses through the Gell-Mann-Oakes-Renner relation:

$$m_\pi^2 f_\pi^2 = -(m_u + m_d) \langle \bar{q} q \rangle$$

where $f_\pi \sim 93$ MeV is the pion decay constant.

The baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta \sim 6 \times 10^{-10}$ must be established before the quark-hadron transition through baryogenesis mechanisms. Electroweak sphaleron processes, which violate baryon and lepton number but conserve $B-L$, erase any pre-existing asymmetry if it does not reside in $B-L$. Leptogenesis generates $B-L$ asymmetry through out-of-equilibrium decay of heavy Majorana neutrinos $N_i \to \ell + H$ (lepton number $+1$) versus $N_i \to \bar{\ell} + H^\dagger$ (lepton number $-1$), with CP violation in the interference between tree-level and one-loop diagrams producing unequal decay rates. Sphalerons then partially convert the lepton asymmetry into baryon asymmetry, yielding:

$$\eta = \frac{n_B - n_{\bar{B}}}{n_\gamma} \sim \frac{a_\text{sph}}{g_*} \epsilon_\text{CP} \kappa$$

where $a_\text{sph} \sim 1/3$ is the sphaleron conversion factor, $g_* \sim 100$ counts relativistic degrees of freedom, $\epsilon_\text{CP}$ is the CP asymmetry parameter, and $\kappa$ is a dilution factor from inverse decays and washout processes.

### Big Bang Nucleosynthesis: Reaction Network Dynamics

BBN begins when the temperature drops to $T \sim 1$ MeV, allowing deuterium formation without immediate photodissociation. The primordial plasma consists of protons, neutrons, electrons, positrons, photons, and neutrinos, with the neutron-to-proton ratio evolving through weak interactions:

$$n \leftrightarrow p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e, \quad n + \nu_e \leftrightarrow p + e^-, \quad n + e^+ \leftrightarrow p + \bar{\nu}_e$$

At high temperatures ($T \gg 1$ MeV), these reactions maintain equilibrium with neutron fraction:

$$\frac{n}{p} = \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta m c^2}{k_B T} \right) \quad \text{where} \quad \Delta m = m_n - m_p = 1.293 \, \text{MeV}/c^2$$

Freeze-out occurs when $\Gamma_\text{weak} \sim G_F^2 T^5 < H \sim T^2 / M_\text{Pl}$, yielding freeze-out temperature $T_f \sim 0.8$ MeV and $n/p|_{T_f} \sim 1/6$. Neutron decay $n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$ with lifetime $\tau_n = 880$ seconds further reduces the ratio to $n/p \sim 1/7$ by the time deuterium formation begins at $T \sim 0.1$ MeV.

The deuterium bottleneck determines nucleosynthesis timing. Deuterium binding energy is $B_D = 2.22$ MeV, but high photon-to-baryon ratio $\eta^{-1} \sim 10^9$ means photons in the Wien tail of the blackbody spectrum photodissociate deuterium through $D + \gamma \to p + n$ until $T$ drops below $T \sim 0.1$ MeV. The Saha equation for deuterium formation:

$$\frac{n_D}{n_p n_n} = \frac{3}{4} \left( \frac{2\pi}{m_N T} \right)^{3/2} \exp\left( \frac{B_D}{T} \right)$$

shows that significant deuterium abundance $X_D \sim 0.01$ requires $T \lesssim 0.1$ MeV. Once deuterium survives, the reaction network proceeds rapidly through:

$$\begin{aligned}
D + p &\to {}^3\text{He} + \gamma \\
D + D &\to {}^3\text{He} + n \\
D + D &\to {}^3\text{H} + p \\
{}^3\text{He} + n &\to {}^4\text{He} + \gamma \\
{}^3\text{H} + p &\to {}^4\text{He} + \gamma \\
{}^3\text{He} + D &\to {}^4\text{He} + p
\end{aligned}$$

Nearly all neutrons end up in helium-4, the most tightly bound light nucleus ($B/A = 7.07$ MeV per nucleon). The helium-4 mass fraction is:

$$Y_p = \frac{2(n/p)}{1 + (n/p)} \sim 0.25$$

with residual deuterium $D/H \sim 2.5 \times 10^{-5}$, helium-3 ${}^3\text{He}/H \sim 10^{-5}$, and lithium-7 ${}^7\text{Li}/H \sim 10^{-10}$. These predictions match primordial abundance measurements from low-metallicity stars and high-redshift quasar absorption systems, providing the strongest empirical confirmation of the hot Big Bang and constraining the baryon density $\Omega_b h^2 = 0.022 \pm 0.001$.

### Recombination and Photon Decoupling: Ionization Kinetics

Recombination transforms the universe from an ionized plasma ($x_e \sim 1$) to neutral hydrogen ($x_e \sim 10^{-3}$), suppressing Thomson scattering $e^- + \gamma \to e^- + \gamma$ and allowing photons to stream freely. The Saha equation provides a first approximation, but accurate modeling requires solving the full Boltzmann hierarchy for ionization kinetics, including:

1. **Photoionization and recombination rates**: The direct recombination rate to the ground state is $\alpha_A(T) \sim 10^{-13} (T/10^4 \, \text{K})^{-0.7} \, \text{cm}^3 \, \text{s}^{-1}$, but emitted Lyman-$\alpha$ photons immediately photoionize neighboring hydrogen atoms, creating a bottleneck. Case B recombination (excluding ground state) dominates, with rate $\alpha_B(T) \sim 0.7 \alpha_A(T)$.

2. **Two-photon decay**: The $2s \to 1s$ transition proceeds via two-photon emission with rate $\Lambda_{2s \to 1s} = 8.23 \, \text{s}^{-1}$, bypassing the Lyman-$\alpha$ bottleneck and accelerating recombination.

3. **Continuum absorption**: Free-free absorption $e^- + p + \gamma \to e^- + p$ redistributes photons in the Rayleigh-Jeans tail, delaying recombination by maintaining a non-thermal distortion.

The ionization fraction evolution is governed by the Peebles equation:

$$\frac{dx_e}{dt} = C \left[ \beta(T_\gamma) (1 - x_e) - n_H \alpha^{(2)}(T_m) x_e^2 \right]$$

where $\beta(T_\gamma)$ is the photoionization rate, $\alpha^{(2)}(T_m)$ is the Case B recombination coefficient, $T_\gamma$ is the photon temperature, $T_m$ is the matter temperature, and $C$ is the Peebles correction factor accounting for non-equilibrium effects. Matter and radiation temperatures decouple when Compton scattering becomes inefficient at $x_e \sim 10^{-2}$, with $T_m$ subsequently evolving adiabatically as $T_m \propto a^{-2}$ (from gravitational heating) rather than $T_\gamma \propto a^{-1}$.

The CMB photosphere corresponds to the last scattering surface at $z_* \sim 1100$, with finite thickness $\Delta z \sim 80$ due to the gradual nature of recombination. The visibility function $g(z) = -d\tau/dz \exp(-\tau)$, where $\tau = \int n_e \sigma_T c \, dt$ is the optical depth, peaks at $z_*$ and has width $\Delta z$. Temperature anisotropies $\Delta T / T$ at angular scale $\theta$ arise from:

1. **Sachs-Wolfe effect**: Gravitational redshift from potential wells $\Phi$, contributing $\Delta T / T \sim \Phi / c^2$ on scales larger than the horizon at last scattering.

2. **Acoustic oscillations**: Pressure-driven oscillations in the photon-baryon fluid before decoupling, producing peaks in the angular power spectrum $C_\ell$ at multipoles $\ell \sim n \pi / \theta_s$, where $\theta_s \sim 1^\circ$ is the sound horizon angle and $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ indexes the harmonic.

3. **Silk damping**: Photon diffusion on scales smaller than the damping scale $\lambda_D \sim (6 / n_e \sigma_T)^{1/2}$ erases anisotropies at high $\ell$, exponentially suppressing power for $\ell \gtrsim 1000$.

The CMB temperature power spectrum measured by Planck satellite:

$$C_\ell = \frac{1}{2\ell + 1} \sum_m |\alpha_{\ell m}|^2$$

where $\Delta T(\hat{n}) = \sum_{\ell m} \alpha_{\ell m} Y_{\ell m}(\hat{n})$ is the spherical harmonic decomposition, constrains six cosmological parameters ($\Omega_b h^2$, $\Omega_c h^2$, $H_0$, $\tau$, $n_s$, $A_s$) with percent-level precision, validating the $\Lambda$CDM concordance model.

### Matter-Radiation Equality and Structure Formation Onset

The equality epoch at $z_\text{eq} \sim 3400$ marks the transition from radiation-dominated expansion $a \propto t^{1/2}$ to matter-dominated expansion $a \propto t^{2/3}$. The energy density ratio:

$$\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_r} = \frac{\Omega_m}{\Omega_r} \left( \frac{a_0}{a} \right) = (1 + z)$$

equals unity at $z_\text{eq}$. During radiation domination, gravitational potential wells oscillate, preventing structure growth. After matter domination, perturbations grow as $\delta \rho / \rho \propto a$ on scales larger than the Jeans length:

$$\lambda_J = c_s \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{G \rho_m}} \sim 20 \left( \frac{\Omega_m h^2}{0.14} \right)^{-1/2} \left( \frac{1 + z}{1000} \right)^{-3/2} \, \text{kpc}$$

where $c_s$ is the sound speed. Cold dark matter (CDM) perturbations grow logarithmically during radiation domination and linearly during matter domination, while baryonic perturbations remain frozen by photon pressure until recombination. After decoupling, baryons fall into CDM potential wells, initiating structure formation.

The matter power spectrum $P(k)$ at redshift $z$ evolves from the primordial spectrum $\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) \propto k^{n_s - 1}$ through the transfer function $T(k)$:

$$P(k, z) = A k^{n_s} T^2(k) D^2(z)$$

where $D(z)$ is the growth factor. On large scales ($k \ll k_\text{eq}$), $T(k) \sim 1$ and the spectrum retains the primordial tilt $n_s \sim 0.965$. On small scales ($k \gg k_\text{eq}$), $T(k) \propto k^{-2} \ln(k)$, suppressing power due to horizon entry during radiation domination. The turnover scale $k_\text{eq} \sim 0.01 \, \text{Mpc}^{-1}$ corresponds to the horizon size at equality. Non-linear structure formation proceeds through gravitational collapse, producing dark matter halos with density profiles $\rho(r) \propto r^{-1}$ (NFW profile) and mass function $dn/d\ln M$ described by Press-Schechter or excursion set theory.

## 3. High-Performance Optimization & Failure Modes

Reconstructing the thermal history from observational data requires solving coupled Boltzmann hierarchies for photons, baryons, dark matter, and neutrinos across 60 orders of magnitude in scale factor ($10^{-30} < a < 1$). The primary computational challenge lies in numerical stability when integrating stiff ordinary differential equations (ODEs) that couple fast oscillatory modes (photon-baryon acoustic oscillations) with slow adiabatic expansion. Modern Boltzmann codes (CAMB, CLASS) employ adaptive timestepping with implicit integrators, spectral decomposition to truncate the infinite Boltzmann hierarchy at high multipoles, and tight-coupling approximations before recombination to collapse the hierarchy into a reduced system.

The photon Boltzmann equation in Fourier space:

$$\frac{\partial \Theta_\ell}{\partial \tau} + ik\mu \Theta_\ell = S_\ell[\Theta, \delta_b, v_b, \Phi, \Psi]$$

couples temperature multipoles $\Theta_\ell(k, \tau)$ through sources $S_\ell$ that include gravitational potentials $\Phi, \Psi$, baryon density $\delta_b$, and velocity $v_b$. The hierarchy truncates at $\ell_\text{max} \sim 10-30$ using line-of-sight integration:

$$\Theta_\ell(k, \tau_0) = \int_0^{\tau_0} S_\ell(k, \tau) j_\ell[k(\tau_0 - \tau)] \, d\tau$$

where $j_\ell$ are spherical Bessel functions. The integral kernel oscillates rapidly for $k(\tau_0 - \tau) \gg 1$, requiring dense sampling or interpolation schemes. Optimized implementations pre-compute Bessel functions on adaptive grids and use FFT-based convolution to evaluate power spectra $C_\ell = (2/\pi) \int k^2 \, |\Theta_\ell(k)|^2 \, P_\Phi(k) \, dk$ efficiently.

BBN reaction network calculations must handle 12 nuclides ($n, p, D, {}^3\text{H}, {}^3\text{He}, {}^4\text{He}, {}^7\text{Li}, {}^7\text{Be}, {}^6\text{Li}, A>7$ groups$) coupled through $\sim 100$ nuclear reactions with rates spanning 20 orders of magnitude. The system of ODEs:

$$\frac{dY_i}{dt} = \sum_j \left( \lambda_{j \to i} Y_j - \lambda_{i \to j} Y_i \right)$$

where $Y_i \equiv n_i / n_B$ is the abundance of species $i$, exhibits stiffness due to fast equilibrium reactions (e.g., $p + n \leftrightarrow D + \gamma$) coupled to slow freeze-out. Implicit integrators (backward differentiation formulas, Rosenbrock methods) with adaptive timestep control handle the stiffness, while nuclear cross-section uncertainties propagate into abundance predictions through Monte Carlo sampling. The dominant uncertainty in primordial lithium-7 arises from the ${}^7\text{Be}(n, p){}^7\text{Li}$ cross-section, with a factor of 2-3 discrepancy between observed lithium in metal-poor stars and BBN predictions—the "lithium problem"—remaining unresolved.

Leptogenesis calculations require solving the Boltzmann equations for heavy neutrino number densities $n_{N_i}$ and lepton asymmetry $n_{B-L}$ in an expanding universe:

$$\frac{dn_{N_i}}{dt} + 3Hn_{N_i} = -\Gamma_D (n_{N_i} - n_{N_i}^\text{eq}) - \Gamma_S (n_{N_i} - n_{N_i}^\text{eq})$$

$$\frac{dn_{B-L}}{dt} + 3Hn_{B-L} = \epsilon_i \Gamma_D (n_{N_i} - n_{N_i}^\text{eq}) - \Gamma_W n_{B-L}$$

where $\Gamma_D$ and $\Gamma_S$ are decay and scattering rates, $\epsilon_i$ is the CP asymmetry, and $\Gamma_W$ is the washout rate. The CP asymmetry involves loop integrals over neutrino Yukawa couplings:

$$\epsilon_i = \frac{1}{8\pi (h^\dagger h)_{ii}} \sum_{j \neq i} \text{Im}\left[ (h^\dagger h)_{ij}^2 \right] f\left( \frac{M_j^2}{M_i^2} \right)$$

where $h$ is the neutrino Yukawa matrix and $f(x)$ is a loop function. Resonant leptogenesis exploits near-degenerate heavy neutrino masses to enhance $\epsilon_i$ by factors of $10^3-10^6$, relaxing the scale of leptogenesis from $M_i \sim 10^{10}$ GeV down to TeV scales accessible to colliders, though this requires fine-tuning the mass splittings $\Delta M / M \sim 10^{-5}$.

Lattice QCD calculations of the quark-gluon plasma equation of state require Monte Carlo sampling of Euclidean path integrals with fermion determinants. The sign problem—oscillatory phases in the determinant at finite baryon chemical potential $\mu_B \neq 0$—prohibits direct Monte Carlo at large $\mu_B$, limiting lattice QCD to $\mu_B / T \lesssim 1$. Analytic continuation from imaginary chemical potential $i\mu_B$ or Taylor expansion around $\mu_B = 0$ partially circumvents this, but the early universe ($\mu_B / T \sim 10^{-9}$ from baryon asymmetry) lies comfortably within the accessible regime. Continuum extrapolation requires simulations at multiple lattice spacings $a$ and extrapolation $a \to 0$ to remove discretization artifacts, with current state-of-the-art achieving $a \sim 0.05$ fm and temperature range $100 \, \text{MeV} < T < 1 \, \text{GeV}$.

Parameter inference from CMB and large-scale structure (LSS) data employs Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling to explore the posterior distribution $P(\theta | D) \propto P(D | \theta) P(\theta)$, where $\theta = (\Omega_b h^2, \Omega_c h^2, H_0, \tau, n_s, A_s)$ are cosmological parameters, $D$ represents CMB power spectra and LSS correlation functions, and $P(D | \theta)$ is the likelihood. The likelihood evaluation requires running a Boltzmann code ($\sim 1$ CPU-second per parameter point), making brute-force grid sampling infeasible. Adaptive MCMC algorithms (Metropolis-Hastings with covariance learning, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo) converge after $\sim 10^5$ likelihood evaluations, with multiple chains run in parallel to verify convergence through Gelman-Rubin statistics. Approximate emulators (Gaussian processes, neural networks trained on Boltzmann code outputs) accelerate likelihood evaluation by factors of $10^3-10^4$, enabling extended parameter spaces including neutrino masses, dark energy equation of state $w(z)$, and early dark energy.

Systematic uncertainties in thermal history reconstruction arise from:

1. **Reionization optical depth**: The integrated optical depth $\tau = \int n_e \sigma_T c \, dt$ from reionization at $z \sim 6-20$ degenerates with primordial amplitude $A_s$, as both affect large-scale CMB polarization. CMB polarization measurements (TE, EE, BB) break this degeneracy, with Planck constraining $\tau = 0.054 \pm 0.007$.

2. **Helium abundance priors**: BBN predictions for $Y_p$ depend on the baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta$ and effective neutrino number $N_\text{eff}$. Independent measurements of $Y_p$ from HII regions provide cross-checks, though systematic uncertainties in stellar models and ionization corrections dominate at the $\sim 1\%$ level.

3. **Neutrino mass constraints**: Massive neutrinos suppress structure formation on scales below the free-streaming length $\lambda_\text{fs} \sim 10 \left( m_\nu / 0.1 \, \text{eV} \right)^{-1}$ Mpc, affecting LSS power spectrum shape. Current limits $\sum m_\nu < 0.12$ eV (95% CL) from Planck+BAO constrain the absolute neutrino mass scale, complementing oscillation experiments that measure only mass-squared differences.

4. **Primordial non-Gaussianity**: Deviations from Gaussian initial conditions, parameterized by $f_\text{NL}$, arise in multi-field inflation or non-canonical kinetic terms. CMB bispectrum measurements constrain $f_\text{NL}^\text{local} = -0.9 \pm 5.1$, ruling out large non-Gaussianity and favoring single-field slow-roll inflation.

Failure modes in thermal history modeling include:

1. **Tight-coupling approximation breakdown**: Before recombination, photon-baryon coupling is strong ($\Gamma_\gamma \gg H$), justifying a fluid approximation. If extended inappropriately into the diffusion regime ($\Gamma_\gamma \sim H$), errors accumulate in damping tail predictions. Multi-level approximations (tight coupling → Boltzmann hierarchy truncation → line-of-sight integration) must transition smoothly at $\tau \sim 1$.

2. **Helium recombination treatment**: Helium recombines before hydrogen ($z_\text{He II} \sim 2000$, $z_\text{He I} \sim 6000$), modifying the ionization history and Compton heating rates. Simplified treatments that ignore helium underestimate $\tau$ by $\sim 10\%$, biasing $A_s$ inference.

3. **Neutrino anisotropic stress**: Massless neutrinos free-stream after decoupling at $T_\nu \sim 1$ MeV, generating anisotropic stress that modifies gravitational potential evolution through $k^2 (\Phi + \Psi) = -8\pi G a^2 \rho_\nu \Theta_{\nu, 2}$. Neglecting this effect overestimates small-scale power by $\sim 5\%$.

4. **Reionization inhomogeneities**: Patchy reionization produces scale-dependent optical depth fluctuations, distorting large-scale polarization power spectra. Homogeneous reionization models approximate the mean optical depth correctly but underestimate TE/EE power on scales $\ell \sim 10-100$ where reionization fluctuations peak.

Observational targets for refining thermal history include:

- **CMB spectral distortions**: Heating from decaying dark matter, primordial black hole evaporation, or dissipation of acoustic waves produces $\mu$-type and $y$-type distortions, detectable at $\Delta I / I \sim 10^{-8}$ sensitivity (PIXIE mission concept).
- **21 cm cosmology**: Hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen at $z \sim 10-200$ traces cosmic dawn and reionization, with differential brightness temperature $\delta T_b \sim 20 \, \text{mK} \, x_\text{HI} (1 + \delta_b)$ encoding baryon density fluctuations before galaxy formation.
- **Primordial gravitational waves**: Inflationary tensor modes produce B-mode polarization in the CMB at $r \equiv P_t / P_s \sim 10^{-3}-10^{-1}$, with detection requiring foreground removal (Galactic dust, synchrotron) at sub-$\mu$K precision.
- **Cosmic neutrino background**: Direct detection of relic neutrinos ($T_\nu = 1.95$ K, $n_\nu \sim 340 \, \text{cm}^{-3}$) requires target masses $\sim$ ton and interaction rates $\sim 1 \, \text{event/year}$, challenging due to $E_\nu \sim 10^{-4}$ eV energy scale and coherent neutrino-nucleus scattering cross-section $\sigma \sim 10^{-56} \, \text{cm}^2$.

The thermal history framework unifies particle physics, nuclear physics, atomic physics, and gravity into a single narrative arc tracing the universe from Planck-scale quantum foam to the cosmic web of galaxies we observe today. Every phase transition—inflation to reheating, electroweak symmetry breaking, quark confinement, nucleosynthesis, recombination—left empirical signatures encoded in the CMB, light element abundances, large-scale structure, and gravitational wave backgrounds. Precision measurements of these relics constrain fundamental physics at energy scales inaccessible to terrestrial experiments, transforming cosmology into a particle physics laboratory where the universe itself is the accelerator.
