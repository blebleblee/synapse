# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Acoustics
## Objective & Prerequisites
### Objective
This lab aims to introduce beginners to the fundamental concepts of acoustics in the context of music production. By the end of this lab, you will have set up your environment, performed a basic experiment, and inspected the results.

### Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of music theory.
- Access to a computer with audio recording capabilities.
- Software: Audacity (free, open-source audio editing software) and a text editor of your choice.
- Microphone (built-in or external).

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check
### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Download and Install Audacity:**
   - Visit the official Audacity website: [Audacity Download](https://www.audacityteam.org/download/)
   - Download the version suitable for your operating system and follow the installation instructions.

2. **Verify Installation:**
   - Open Audacity to ensure it launches correctly.
   - Check that your microphone is recognized by Audacity:
     - Go to `Edit` > `Preferences` > `Devices`.
     - Ensure your microphone is listed under the `Recording` section.
     - Test the microphone by clicking the `Test` button and speaking into it. You should see the waveform change in the Audacity interface.

### Expected Output
- Audacity opens without errors.
- Microphone is recognized and tested successfully.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment
### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Record a Sound:**
   - Click the `Record` button (red circle) in Audacity.
   - Speak into the microphone or clap your hands.
   - Click the `Stop` button (yellow square) to end the recording.

2. **Play Back the Recording:**
   - Click the `Play` button (triangle) to listen to your recording.

3. **Save the Recording:**
   - Go to `File` > `Export` > `Export as WAV...`.
   - Choose a location to save your file and click `Save`.

### Expected Output
- You should hear the sound you recorded when you play it back.
- The recording should be saved as a WAV file in the specified location.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State
### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **View the Waveform:**
   - Observe the waveform in the Audacity interface. This visual representation shows the amplitude of the sound over time.

2. **Check the Frequency Spectrum:**
   - Go to `Analyze` > `Plot Spectrum`.
   - A new window will open displaying the frequency spectrum of your recording.

### Expected Output
- You should see a waveform that corresponds to the sound you recorded.
- The frequency spectrum should show peaks at frequencies present in your recording.

## Challenge Exercise
### Objective
Perform a simple self-test to ensure you understand the basics of recording and analyzing sound.

### Instructions
1. **Record a Short Tone:**
   - Use a tuning fork or a musical instrument to produce a single, clear tone.
   - Record this tone in Audacity.

2. **Analyze the Tone:**
   - Use the `Plot Spectrum` feature to analyze the recorded tone.
   - Identify the frequency of the tone based on the peak in the frequency spectrum.

### Expected Output
- You should be able to identify the frequency of the tone you recorded by looking at the peak in the frequency spectrum.

---

This concludes the Beginner Hands-On Lab for Acoustics. You now have a foundational understanding of setting up your environment, recording sound, and analyzing it using Audacity.
