# Beginner Hands-On Lab: MIDI
## Objective & Prerequisites
**Objective:**  
In this lab, you will set up a basic MIDI environment, execute your first MIDI command, and inspect the basic state of your setup. By the end of this lab, you should be able to send and receive MIDI messages using a simple MIDI controller and software.

**Prerequisites:**  
- A computer with internet access.
- A MIDI controller (such as a keyboard, drum pad, or MIDI fader).
- A DAW (Digital Audio Workstation) like Ableton Live, FL Studio, or GarageBand.
- Basic understanding of MIDI concepts (optional but recommended).

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check (Exact commands to verify tools)
1. **Install a DAW:**  
   - Download and install a DAW of your choice from their official website.
   - Launch the DAW and ensure it is running correctly.

2. **Connect your MIDI Controller:**  
   - Physically connect your MIDI controller to your computer using a USB cable.
   - Ensure your MIDI controller is powered on.

3. **Verify MIDI Connection in DAW:**  
   - In your DAW, navigate to the MIDI settings or preferences.
   - Check that your MIDI controller is recognized and listed as an input device.
   - If not, refer to the DAW's manual or online resources for troubleshooting.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment (Step-by-step guided instructions with expected outputs)
1. **Open a New Project:**  
   - Create a new project in your DAW.

2. **Insert a MIDI Instrument:**  
   - Insert a MIDI instrument track (e.g., a software synthesizer) into your project.
   - Ensure the MIDI instrument is set to receive MIDI messages from your controller.

3. **Play a Note:**  
   - Press a key on your MIDI controller.
   - You should hear a sound from the MIDI instrument in your DAW.
   - This confirms that your MIDI controller is sending messages and your DAW is receiving them.

4. **Verify MIDI Messages:**  
   - In your DAW, look for a MIDI editor or piano roll view.
   - Play a few notes on your MIDI controller.
   - You should see the notes appear in the MIDI editor, indicating that MIDI messages are being recorded.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State (Reading logs or simple outputs)
1. **Check MIDI Input:**  
   - In your DAW, navigate to the MIDI input section.
   - Ensure that your MIDI controller is selected as the input device.
   - Play a note on your MIDI controller and verify that the input is being received.

2. **Check MIDI Output:**  
   - In your DAW, navigate to the MIDI output section.
   - Ensure that the MIDI instrument is set as the output device.
   - Play a note on your MIDI controller and verify that the output is being sent to the MIDI instrument.

## Challenge Exercise (Simple self-test for beginners)
1. **Create a Simple Melody:**  
   - Using your MIDI controller, create a simple melody by playing a series of notes.
   - Record the melody in the MIDI editor.
   - Play back the recorded melody to ensure it sounds as expected.

2. **Adjust MIDI Settings:**  
   - Experiment with different MIDI settings such as velocity, aftertouch, and control changes.
   - Observe how these settings affect the sound of the MIDI instrument.

By completing this lab, you should have a basic understanding of setting up a MIDI environment, sending and receiving MIDI messages, and using a DAW to work with MIDI.
