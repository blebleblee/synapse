# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Beatmaking

## Objective & Prerequisites

### Objective
The objective of this lab is to introduce you to the foundational tools and processes of beatmaking. By the end of this lab, you will have set up a basic beatmaking environment, created your first drum pattern, and learned how to inspect the basic state of your project.

### Prerequisites
- A computer with a modern operating system (Windows, macOS, or Linux).
- Basic familiarity with using a computer and navigating file systems.
- Access to a digital audio workstation (DAW) such as Ableton Live, FL Studio, or GarageBand. For this lab, we will use **Ableton Live Lite**, which is free and available for download from the Ableton website.

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check

### Step 1: Download and Install Ableton Live Lite
1. Visit the [Ableton Live Lite download page](https://www.ableton.com/en/products/live-lite/).
2. Download the installer for your operating system.
3. Run the installer and follow the on-screen instructions to complete the installation.

### Step 2: Launch Ableton Live Lite
1. Open Ableton Live Lite from your applications folder or desktop shortcut.
2. Upon launching, Ableton Live Lite should open to a new project with a default set of instruments and effects.

### Step 3: Verify Installation
1. Ensure that Ableton Live Lite is running without any errors.
2. Check that the default instruments and effects are available and functional.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment

### Step 1: Create a New Project
1. In Ableton Live Lite, go to `File` > `New` to create a new project.
2. A new project window will open with a default set of tracks.

### Step 2: Add a Drum Rack
1. Click on the `Add Track` button at the bottom of the session view to add a new track.
2. Click on the `Instrument` button in the new track to open the instrument selector.
3. Scroll down and select `Drum Rack`. Click `OK` to add the Drum Rack to the track.

### Step 3: Load a Drum Kit
1. In the Drum Rack, click on the `Browse` button to open the library.
2. Navigate to `Drum Kits` and select a kit, such as `Kick & Snare`. Click `OK` to load the kit.

### Step 4: Create a Simple Drum Pattern
1. In the Drum Rack, you will see a grid of pads representing different drum sounds.
2. Click on the `Session View` button at the top of the screen to switch to session view.
3. Click on the `Clip` button in the first slot of the track to create a new clip.
4. Click on the `Step Sequencer` button to enter step sequencer mode.
5. Click on the pads in the step sequencer to create a simple drum pattern. For example, click on the kick drum pad (usually the bottom-left pad) on every beat, and the snare drum pad (usually the middle-left pad) on beats 2 and 4.

### Step 5: Play Your Drum Pattern
1. Click on the play button (triangle icon) at the top of the screen to start playing the drum pattern.
2. You should hear the drum pattern you created.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State

### Step 1: View the Clip
1. In session view, click on the clip you created to view its contents.
2. You should see the step sequencer with the drum pattern you created.

### Step 2: Check the Drum Rack
1. Click on the `Drum Rack` device in the track to open it.
2. Ensure that the drum kit you loaded is displayed and that the pads are correctly mapped to the drum sounds.

## Challenge Exercise

### Task
Create a simple bassline using the `Ableton Operator` synthesizer.

### Steps
1. Add a new track by clicking the `Add Track` button.
2. Click on the `Instrument` button in the new track and select `Operator`.
3. Click on the `Session View` button to switch to session view.
4. Click on the `Clip` button in the first slot of the track to create a new clip.
5. Click on the `Step Sequencer` button to enter step sequencer mode.
6. Create a simple bassline by clicking on the pads in the step sequencer. You can use the default settings for the Operator synthesizer or experiment with different settings to create different sounds.
7. Play the clip to hear your bassline.

### Expected Outcome
You should hear a simple bassline playing along with the drum pattern you created in Exercise 2.

---

By completing this lab, you have successfully set up a basic beatmaking environment, created your first drum pattern, and experimented with creating a simple bassline. This foundational knowledge will serve as a starting point for more advanced beatmaking techniques.
