# Operational Verification Labs: 03 Pentatonic Modes And Blues Phrasing

## Objective & Lab Setup

### Objective
The objective of this laboratory is to provide hands-on experience with the foundational pentatonic modes and blues phrasing on the electric guitar. Through a series of exercises, you will explore the CAGED fretboard system, practice modal soloing, and incorporate blues phrasing techniques. This lab will enhance your understanding of scale theory, improvisation, and the expressive capabilities of the electric guitar.

### Lab Setup
- **Electric Guitar**: Ensure your guitar is properly tuned to standard EADGBE tuning.
- **Amp and Effects Pedals**: Use an amplifier and any desired effects pedals (e.g., overdrive, distortion, delay) to shape your tone.
- **Metronome**: Use a metronome to maintain a consistent tempo.
- **Tablature Software**: Use tablature software or a digital audio workstation (DAW) to visualize and record your exercises (e.g., Guitar Pro, TuxGuitar, Ableton Live).
- **Recording Device**: Use a recording device to capture your practice sessions for analysis and improvement.

## Exercise 1: Foundational Practical Implementation

### Exercise 1.1: CAGED Fretboard System Overview
The CAGED system is a method for visualizing the fretboard by breaking it down into five distinct patterns, each corresponding to a different open chord shape. These patterns are based on the open chords C, A, G, E, and D.

#### Step-by-Step Implementation
1. **Identify the C Pattern**:
   - Locate the C major chord shape on the 8th fret.
   - Visualize the C pentatonic scale pattern starting from the 8th fret.
   - Practice ascending and descending the C pentatonic scale using the C pattern.

2. **Identify the A Pattern**:
   - Locate the A major chord shape on the 5th fret.
   - Visualize the A pentatonic scale pattern starting from the 5th fret.
   - Practice ascending and descending the A pentatonic scale using the A pattern.

3. **Identify the G Pattern**:
   - Locate the G major chord shape on the 3rd fret.
   - Visualize the G pentatonic scale pattern starting from the 3rd fret.
   - Practice ascending and descending the G pentatonic scale using the G pattern.

4. **Identify the E Pattern**:
   - Locate the E major chord shape on the 0th fret.
   - Visualize the E pentatonic scale pattern starting from the 0th fret.
   - Practice ascending and descending the E pentatonic scale using the E pattern.

5. **Identify the D Pattern**:
   - Locate the D major chord shape on the 10th fret.
   - Visualize the D pentatonic scale pattern starting from the 10th fret.
   - Practice ascending and descending the D pentatonic scale using the D pattern.

### Exercise 1.2: Modal Soloing
Practice modal soloing using the C pentatonic scale in the key of C major.

#### Step-by-Step Implementation

## Practical Cheat Sheet & Quick Reference

### CAGED Fretboard System Patterns
- **C Pattern**: C major chord on the 8th fret.
- **A Pattern**: A major chord on the 5th fret.
- **G Pattern**: G major chord on the 3rd fret.
- **E Pattern**: E major chord on the 0th fret.
- **D Pattern**: D major chord on the 10th fret.

### Pentatonic Scale Patterns
- **C Pentatonic**: C, D, E, G, A
- **A Pentatonic**: A, C, D, E, G
- **G Pentatonic**: G, A, B, D, E
- **E Pentatonic**: E, F#, G#, B, C#
- **D Pentatonic**: D, F, G, A, C

### Blues Phrasing Techniques
- **Bending**: Bend a note upward to create a bluesy sound.
- **Hammer-ons**: Strike a string with a finger while another finger is already pressing down on the string.
- **Pull-offs**: Release a finger from a string while another finger is pressing down on the string.
- **Slides**: Slide a finger up or down the fretboard to create a smooth transition between notes.

### Metronome Settings
- **Moderate Tempo**: 120 BPM
- **Fast Tempo**: 160 BPM

### Recording Tips
- Use a high-quality recording device.
- Capture clear audio with minimal background noise.
- Listen back to your recordings for analysis and improvement.
