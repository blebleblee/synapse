# Beginner Technical Lesson: Electric Guitar
## 1. Core Mechanics & Basic Principles

The electric guitar is a stringed musical instrument that produces sound when a string is plucked or strummed. The sound is then amplified through an electric pickup and a speaker. The core mechanics of an electric guitar can be broken down into the following components:

* **Strings:** The strings are the vibrating elements that produce sound. There are typically 6 strings on a standard electric guitar, tuned to specific notes.
* **Fretboard:** The fretboard is the long, flat piece of wood glued to the neck of the guitar. It has raised bars called frets that divide the neck into different segments.
* **Neck:** The neck is the long, thin part of the guitar that connects the headstock to the body.
* **Body:** The body is the main resonating chamber of the guitar, responsible for amplifying the sound.
* **Pickups:** The pickups are magnetic devices that capture the vibrations of the strings and convert them into an electrical signal.
* **Tuning Machines:** The tuning machines are used to tune the strings to the desired pitch.

## 2. Step-by-Step Component Breakdown

### 2.1 Strings

* **String Types:** There are two main types of strings: nickel-wound and stainless steel.
* **String Gauge:** The gauge of the string refers to its thickness. Thicker strings produce a fuller sound, while thinner strings produce a brighter sound.
* **String Tuning:** The standard tuning for an electric guitar is EADGBE.

### 2.2 Fretboard

* **Fretboard Materials:** Fretboards are typically made from rosewood, ebony, or maple.
* **Fret Spacing:** The distance between frets is typically 0.9-1.0 inches (2.3-2.5 cm).
* **Fretboard Radius:** The radius of the fretboard refers to its curvature. A flatter radius makes it easier to play chords, while a more curved radius makes it easier to play single notes.

### 2.3 Neck

* **Neck Materials:** Necks are typically made from mahogany, maple, or walnut.
* **Neck Width:** The width of the neck affects the playability of the guitar. A narrower neck is easier to play, while a wider neck is more stable.
* **Neck Profile:** The profile of the neck refers to its shape. A rounded profile is more comfortable to play, while a flat profile is more stable.

### 2.4 Body

* **Body Shapes:** There are several body shapes, including Stratocaster, Les Paul, and Telecaster.
* **Body Materials:** Bodies are typically made from mahogany, ash, or alder.
* **Body Size:** The size of the body affects the resonance and tone of the guitar. A larger body produces a fuller sound, while a smaller body produces a brighter sound.

### 2.5 Pickups

* **Pickup Types:** There are several types of pickups, including single-coil, humbucker, and P-90.
* **Pickup Placement:** The placement of the pickups affects the tone of the guitar. Bridge pickups produce a brighter sound, while neck pickups produce a warmer sound.

### 2.6 Tuning Machines

* **Tuning Machine Types:** There are several types of tuning machines, including machine heads and locking tuners.
* **Tuning Machine Functionality:** Tuning machines are used to tune the strings to the desired pitch.

## 4. What Can Go Wrong

### 4.1 Common Mistakes

* **Incorrect string tuning:** Tuning the strings to the wrong pitch can produce a poor sound.
* **Incorrect pickup placement:** Placing the pickups in the wrong position can affect the tone of the guitar.
* **Incorrect tuning machine configuration:** Using the wrong type of tuning machine can affect the tuning stability.

### 4.2 Spotting Common Mistakes

* **Check the string tuning:** Make sure the strings are tuned to the correct pitch.
* **Check the pickup placement:** Make sure the pickups are placed in the correct position.
* **Check the tuning machine configuration:** Make sure the tuning machines are set up correctly.
