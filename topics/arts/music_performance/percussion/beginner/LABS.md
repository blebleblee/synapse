# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Percussion
## Objective & Prerequisites
### Objective
The objective of this lab is to introduce you to the basics of percussion instruments and to set up a simple environment where you can start experimenting with basic percussion techniques. By the end of this lab, you should be able to identify different percussion instruments, understand their basic playing techniques, and perform a simple rhythm pattern.

### Prerequisites
- Access to a basic percussion kit or individual instruments such as a snare drum, cymbals, and a bass drum.
- A comfortable space to set up and play the instruments.
- Basic understanding of musical notation (optional but recommended).

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check
### Setting Up Your Percussion Kit
1. **Arrange Your Instruments:** Place your percussion instruments in a comfortable playing position. Ensure that the snare drum is stable and the cymbals are securely mounted.
2. **Check Your Sticks:** Make sure you have a pair of drumsticks that fit comfortably in your hands. Test the sticks by tapping them lightly on the snare drum to ensure they produce a clear sound.

### Sanity Check
- **Sound Test:** Tap each instrument to ensure they produce a clear and consistent sound. Adjust the tension on the snare drum if necessary to achieve a crisp sound.
- **Comfort Check:** Ensure that your playing position is comfortable. Adjust the height and angle of the snare drum as needed.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment
### Step-by-Step Guide to Playing a Simple Rhythm
1. **Basic Stance:** Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. Hold the drumsticks in your hands, one in each, with a grip that feels natural and comfortable.
2. **Warm-Up:** Begin with a simple warm-up exercise. Tap the snare drum with one stick, alternating between the right and left hand, at a steady tempo.
3. **Simple Rhythm Pattern:** Practice the following rhythm pattern:
   - **Pattern:** Right stick on snare, left stick on snare, right stick on snare, left stick on snare, right stick on snare, left stick on snare, right stick on snare, left stick on snare.
   - **Timing:** Start slowly and gradually increase the speed as you become more comfortable with the pattern.
4. **Incorporate Cymbals:** Add a crash cymbal on the fourth beat of each measure. The pattern should now be:
   - **Pattern:** Right stick on snare, left stick on snare, right stick on snare, crash cymbal with left stick.

### Expected Outputs
- **Consistent Sound:** Each tap on the snare drum should produce a clear and consistent sound.
- **Rhythm Accuracy:** The rhythm pattern should be played accurately with a steady tempo.
- **Cymbal Crash:** The crash cymbal should be played on the fourth beat of each measure, adding a dynamic element to the rhythm.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State
### Reading and Understanding Your Performance
1. **Listen to Your Rhythm:** Pay attention to the sound of your rhythm. Ensure that each note is clear and that the timing is consistent.
2. **Adjust as Needed:** If you notice any inconsistencies, adjust your grip, stance, or technique to improve the sound and timing.
3. **Practice Regularly:** Regular practice is key to improving your percussion skills. Set aside time each day to practice your rhythm patterns and explore new techniques.

## Challenge Exercise (Simple Self-Test for Beginners)
### Create a Simple Variation
- **Task:** Create a simple variation of the rhythm pattern you learned in Exercise 2. You can add a different cymbal, change the timing, or introduce a new instrument.
- **Guidelines:** Ensure that your new pattern is still easy to follow and maintains a steady tempo.
- **Self-Assessment:** Play your new pattern and assess whether it sounds good and is consistent. Make adjustments as needed.

By completing this lab, you should have a basic understanding of how to set up and play a simple rhythm pattern on percussion instruments. Keep practicing and experimenting to improve your skills and explore new rhythms and techniques.
