# Beginner Technical Lesson: Vocals
## 1. Core Mechanics & Basic Principles
### How it works under the hood at a fundamental level
Singing is a complex process that involves the coordination of several anatomical structures. The primary components include:

- **Diaphragm:** The main muscle used for breathing and controlling the flow of air.
- **Larynx (Voice Box):** Contains the vocal folds (vocal cords) that vibrate to produce sound.
- **Vocal Folds:** Two folds of tissue that vibrate when air passes through them, creating sound.
- **Resonance Cavities:** Spaces in the head and throat that amplify and modify the sound produced by the vocal folds.

### Basic Principles
- **Breath Control:** Efficient use of the diaphragm to maintain a steady flow of air.
- **Vocal Placement:** Directing the sound to resonate in the appropriate cavities for clarity and projection.
- **Warm-up:** Exercises to prepare the vocal cords and muscles for singing.

## 2. Step-by-Step Component Breakdown
### Step 1: Diaphragmatic Breathing
- **Exercise:** Lie down on your back with one hand on your chest and the other on your abdomen. Inhale deeply through your nose, allowing your abdomen to rise while your chest remains still. Exhale slowly through your mouth, feeling your abdomen lower.
- **Purpose:** Develops control over the diaphragm, providing a steady supply of air for singing.

### Step 2: Vocal Warm-up
- **Exercise:** Begin with humming (mm-mm-mm) to relax the vocal cords. Gradually move to lip trills (blowing air through pursed lips to create a buzzing sound) and then to sirens (sliding up and down in pitch).
- **Purpose:** Prepares the vocal cords for singing and helps prevent strain.

### Step 3: Vocal Placement
- **Exercise:** Practice singing a simple tone (like "ah") and focus on feeling the vibration in different parts of your head and throat. Try to place the sound in the mask (the area around the eyes and nose) for clarity.
- **Purpose:** Helps in directing the sound to resonate in the appropriate cavities, enhancing the quality of the voice.

### Step 4: Basic Vocal Techniques
- **Exercise:** Practice singing scales (do-re-mi-fa-so-la-ti-do) in a comfortable range, focusing on maintaining a steady breath and clear tone.
- **Purpose:** Develops control over pitch and tone, essential for singing accurately and expressively.

## 3. First Working Implementation
### Complete, clean, commented starter code or configuration
Below is a simple vocal warm-up routine you can follow daily:

1. **Diaphragmatic Breathing (5 minutes)**
   - Inhale deeply through your nose, allowing your abdomen to rise.
   - Exhale slowly through your mouth, feeling your abdomen lower.
   - Repeat 10 times.

2. **Humming (2 minutes)**
   - Hum a simple tone (mm-mm-mm) for 2 minutes.
   - Focus on relaxing your vocal cords.

3. **Lip Trills (2 minutes)**
   - Pucker your lips and blow air through them to create a buzzing sound.
   - Continue for 2 minutes, focusing on a steady breath.

4. **Sirens (2 minutes)**
   - Slide up in pitch (do-re-mi-fa-so-la-ti) and then back down (ti-la-so-fa-mi-re-do).
   - Repeat 5 times, focusing on a smooth transition between notes.

5. **Vocal Placement (2 minutes)**
   - Sing a simple tone (ah) and focus on feeling the vibration in your mask.
   - Practice for 2 minutes, adjusting your placement as needed.

6. **Scales (5 minutes)**
   - Sing a scale (do-re-mi-fa-so-la-ti-do) in a comfortable range.
   - Focus on maintaining a steady breath and clear tone.
   - Repeat 3 times.

## 4. What Can Go Wrong (Simple common mistakes and how to spot them)
- **Tightness in the Throat:** If you feel tension in your throat while singing, it may indicate incorrect vocal placement. Focus on relaxing your throat and directing the sound to your mask.
- **Breathiness:** If your voice sounds breathy, you may not be using enough breath support. Practice diaphragmatic breathing to ensure a steady flow of air.
- **Strain:** If you feel pain or discomfort in your voice, you may be pushing too hard. Take breaks and focus on proper technique.
