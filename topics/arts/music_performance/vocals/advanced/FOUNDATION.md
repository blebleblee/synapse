## 1. Theoretical Invariants & Formal Frameworks

### Formal Proofs in Vocal Performance
In the domain of vocal performance, formal proofs can be constructed around the invariance of certain physical and physiological principles. For instance, the invariance of the speed of sound in a medium (air, in this case) can be used to derive formal proofs about the propagation of vocal sounds. These proofs can be extended to include the invariance of the fundamental frequency of a note across different environments, assuming constant temperature and pressure.

### Kernel or Physical Invariants
Physical invariants in vocal performance include the invariance of the vocal folds' vibration frequency in producing a specific pitch, regardless of the intensity or volume of the sound. This invariance is crucial for understanding how the human voice can maintain pitch accuracy under varying conditions.

Hardware constraints in vocal performance refer to the physical limitations of the human vocal apparatus, such as the range of the vocal folds and the resonant properties of the vocal tract. These constraints are invariant across different individuals and can be used to derive formal models of vocal performance.

### Axiomatic Definitions
Axiomatic definitions in vocal performance include the invariance of certain physiological principles, such as the relationship between the vocal folds' vibration frequency and the pitch of the produced sound. These definitions form the foundation of vocal performance theory and can be used to derive formal proofs about vocal behavior.
