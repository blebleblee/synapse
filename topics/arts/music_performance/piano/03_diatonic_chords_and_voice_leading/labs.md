# Operational Verification Labs: 03 Diatonic Chords And Voice Leading

## Objective & Lab Setup

### Objective
The objective of this laboratory is to gain a practical understanding of diatonic chords and voice leading through hands-on piano performance exercises. This will involve foundational chord construction, parameter variation, and fault injection to ensure a comprehensive grasp of the concepts.

### Lab Setup
- **Required Tools:** A piano or digital piano, a music notation software (such as MuseScore or Finale), and a metronome (physical or digital).
- **Environment:** A quiet practice space where you can focus on the exercises without distractions.
- **Packages/Simulators:** No additional software packages are required beyond the notation software and metronome.

## Exercise 1: Foundational Practical Implementation

### Exercise Overview
This exercise will focus on constructing and playing diatonic chords in the key of C major. You will practice voice leading between these chords to understand how notes move smoothly from one chord to the next.

### Steps

1. **Chord Construction:**
   - Open your music notation software and create a new score in the key of C major.
   - Construct the following chords: C major (C-E-G), F major (F-A-C), G major (G-B-D), and Am minor (A-C-E).
   - Write these chords in root position in the right hand.

2. **Voice Leading:**
   - Write the chords in the following sequence: C major, F major, G major, Am minor, and return to C major.
   - Practice voice leading between these chords by moving each note to the nearest note in the next chord. For example, move from C-E-G to F-A-C by moving C to F, E to A, and G to C.
   - Ensure that the voice leading is smooth and that no voice leaps more than a third unless necessary.

3. **Performance:**
   - Use a metronome set to a moderate tempo (60-80 BPM) to play the sequence of chords with proper voice leading.
   - Focus on maintaining a steady rhythm and clear articulation of each note.

## Exercise 2: Parameter Variation & Empirical Analysis

### Exercise Overview
In this exercise, you will vary the parameters of the chords and analyze the resulting voice leading. This will help you understand the impact of different chord inversions and voice leading techniques.

### Steps

1. **Chord Inversions:**
   - Rewrite the sequence of chords (C major, F major, G major, Am minor, C major) using different inversions.
   - For example, use the first inversion of C major (E-G-C), the second inversion of F major (A-C-F), and so on.
   - Practice voice leading between these inverted chords, ensuring smooth transitions.

2. **Voice Leading Techniques:**
   - Experiment with different voice leading techniques, such as using passing tones, neighbor tones, and chromatic approach tones.
   - For example, add a passing tone between the F major and G major chords by playing F-A-B-C.
   - Analyze how these techniques affect the smoothness and clarity of the voice leading.

3. **Performance and Analysis:**
   - Use the metronome to play the sequence of chords with the varied inversions and techniques.
   - Record your performance and listen back to analyze the effectiveness of the voice leading.
   - Make adjustments as necessary to improve the smoothness and clarity of the transitions.

## Exercise 3: Fault Injection & Stress Testing

### Exercise Overview
This exercise will involve inducing boundary failures in your voice leading and analyzing the resulting error states. This will help you understand how to recover from mistakes and maintain musical integrity.

### Steps

1. **Inducing Faults:**
   - Introduce deliberate mistakes in your voice leading, such as large leaps between notes or dissonant intervals.
   - For example, move from C-E-G to F-A-C by moving C to A, E to F, and G to C.
   - Practice playing the sequence of chords with these induced faults.

2. **Analyzing Error States:**
   - Use the metronome to play the sequence of chords with the induced faults.
   - Listen carefully to identify the points where the voice leading becomes unclear or dissonant.
   - Analyze the cause of these errors and determine how they can be corrected.

3. **Recovery and Verification:**
   - Correct the induced faults by adjusting the voice leading to ensure smooth transitions.
   - Play the sequence of chords again with the corrected voice leading.
   - Verify that the voice leading is now smooth and clear, and that any mistakes have been successfully recovered.

## Practical Cheat Sheet & Quick Reference

### Chord Voicings in C Major
- **C Major (Root Position):** C-E-G
- **C Major (1st Inversion):** E-G-C
- **C Major (2nd Inversion):** G-C-E
- **F Major (Root Position):** F-A-C
- **F Major (1st Inversion):** A-C-F
- **F Major (2nd Inversion):** C-F-A
- **G Major (Root Position):** G-B-D
- **G Major (1st Inversion):** B-D-G
- **G Major (2nd Inversion):** D-G-B
- **Am Minor (Root Position):** A-C-E
- **Am Minor (1st Inversion):** C-E-A
- **Am Minor (2nd Inversion):** E-A-C

### Voice Leading Techniques
- **Passing Tones:** Notes that connect two chord tones smoothly.
- **Neighbor Tones:** Notes that are approached and left by step from a chord tone.
- **Chromatic Approach Tones:** Notes that approach a chord tone by half step.

### Verification Checks
- **Smooth Transitions:** Ensure that each note moves to the nearest note in the next chord.
- **Clarity:** Avoid dissonant intervals and large leaps between notes.
- **Rhythm:** Maintain a steady rhythm using the metronome.
- **Articulation:** Clear articulation of each note to ensure clarity in voice leading.

By completing these exercises, you will gain a deeper understanding of diatonic chords and voice leading, and be able to apply these techniques in your piano performance.
