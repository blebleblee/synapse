# Lesson 4: Repertoire and Practice

## 1. First-Principles Intuition & Invariants

As pianists, we often overlook the fundamental connection between our physical actions and the resulting sound. The piano, an instrument of precision and nuance, demands a deep understanding of its mechanical and acoustic properties. Let us begin by examining the piano's internal workings, where the harmony between human motion and sound production is forged.

The piano's action, comprising hammers, strings, and escapement mechanisms, is a marvel of mechanical engineering. Each keystroke triggers a cascade of events: the hammer strikes the string, generating a sound wave that resonates within the piano's soundboard and body. The piano's acoustic properties, including its resonant frequencies and soundboard stiffness, significantly influence the tone produced.

To develop a robust repertoire, pianists must internalize these first-principles relationships. By understanding the piano's mechanical and acoustic invariants, we can optimize our practice and performance strategies. For instance, recognizing the piano's resonant frequencies allows us to exploit these properties in our playing, enhancing tone and expressiveness.
