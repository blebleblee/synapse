## 2. Key Architecture & Mechanisms

The architecture of walking lines and harmonic theory is deeply rooted in the interaction between chord progressions, scales, and the techniques used to navigate between them. At the heart of this architecture lies harmonic theory, which involves understanding chord structures, scales, and their relationships. Chord progressions, such as I-IV-V, ii-V-I, and their variations, serve as the backbone of harmonic structures, providing a framework for the bass player to create walking lines. These progressions are constructed using various scale types, including major, minor, blues, dorian, and mixolydian scales, each offering a unique set of notes that can be used to create melodic lines that outline the harmony. Walking lines, on the other hand, are techniques for creating melodic lines that move smoothly between chords, typically using stepwise motion. This stepwise motion is a key mechanism that ensures the walking line remains consistent with the underlying harmonic progression, providing a sense of continuity and stability. Additionally, the use of chord extensions, such as 7ths, 9ths, 11ths, and 13ths, allows for greater harmonic complexity and nuance, enabling the bass player to create more sophisticated walking lines that complement the overall musical texture.
