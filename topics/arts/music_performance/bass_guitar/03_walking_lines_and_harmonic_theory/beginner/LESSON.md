# Beginner Technical Lesson: Bass Guitar 03 Walking Lines And Harmonic Theory

## 1. Core Mechanics & Basic Principles (How it works under the hood at a fundamental level)

**Walking Lines:** Walking lines are a fundamental technique in bass playing, especially in jazz. They are a series of notes that move stepwise (up or down by a half or whole step) or by leaps, creating a smooth, continuous line that outlines the harmony of a piece of music. Walking lines are essential for providing a solid rhythmic foundation and connecting harmonic changes smoothly.

**Harmonic Theory:** Understanding harmonic theory is crucial for creating walking lines that fit well with the music. In Western music, harmony is built on chords, which are groups of notes played simultaneously. Chords are constructed from scales, and the most common scales used in jazz are the major, minor, and modal scales. Chords progressions are sequences of chords that move from one to another, and these progressions are the backbone of most songs.

## 2. Step-by-Step Component Breakdown

### Step 1: Understand the Chord Progression
- Identify the chords in the piece you are playing.
- Determine the key signature and the scale(s) associated with the chords.

### Step 2: Learn Basic Walking Line Patterns
- **Stepwise Movement:** Move up or down the scale by half or whole steps.
- **Leap Movement:** Move up or down by a third, fourth, or fifth.
- **Chromatic Movement:** Move up or down by half steps, including sharps and flats.

### Step 3: Practice with Simple Chord Progressions
- Start with simple chord progressions like I-IV-V-I in a major key.
- Use the scale associated with the key to create your walking lines.

### Step 4: Incorporate Chord Tones
- Use the notes of the chords in your walking lines to emphasize the harmony.
- Practice targeting chord tones on strong beats (beats 1 and 3).

### Step 5: Add Rhythmic Variation
- Experiment with different rhythms to keep your lines interesting.
- Use syncopation and off-beats to add variety.

## 3. First Working Implementation (Complete, clean, commented starter code or configuration)

Let's create a simple walking line for a I-IV-V-I chord progression in the key of C major.

**Chord Progression:**
- C major (C, E, G)
- F major (F, A, C)
- G major (G, B, D)
- C major (C, E, G)

**Walking Line Example:**
- **Bar 1 (C major):** C, D, E, F
- **Bar 2 (F major):** F, G, A, Bb
- **Bar 3 (G major):** G, A, B, C
- **Bar 4 (C major):** C, D, E, C

**Explanation:**
- **Bar 1:** Start on the root of the C major chord (C) and move stepwise up to F.
- **Bar 2:** Start on the root of the F major chord (F) and move stepwise up to Bb, which is the flat seventh of the F major chord.
- **Bar 3:** Start on the root of the G major chord (G) and move stepwise up to C, which is the root of the next C major chord.
- **Bar 4:** Start on the root of the C major chord (C) and move stepwise up to C, creating a resolution.

## 4. What Can Go Wrong (Simple common mistakes and how to spot them)

- **Mistake 1:** Not following the chord progression.
  - **Spotting It:** Your line doesn't align with the chords being played.
  - **Fix:** Practice identifying chord changes and adjust your line accordingly.

- **Mistake 2:** Overusing the same patterns.
  - **Spotting It:** Your lines sound repetitive and lack variety.
  - **Fix:** Experiment with different rhythms and patterns to keep your lines interesting.

- **Mistake 3:** Not emphasizing chord tones.
  - **Spotting It:** Your line doesn't highlight the important notes of the chords.
  - **Fix:** Practice targeting chord tones on strong beats to emphasize the harmony.
