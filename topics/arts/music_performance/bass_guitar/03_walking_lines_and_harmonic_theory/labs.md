# Operational Verification Labs: 03 Walking Lines And Harmonic Theory

## Objective & Lab Setup

### Objective
This laboratory aims to deepen your understanding of walking bass lines and their harmonic foundations. Through practical exercises, you will learn to construct walking bass lines over various chord progressions, analyze their harmonic functions, and explore the rhythmic and melodic nuances that define this essential bass technique.

### Lab Setup
For this lab, you will need the following tools and environments:
- A bass guitar (electric or acoustic) equipped with a pickup and amplifier.
- A metronome (digital or mechanical).
- Music notation software (such as MuseScore, Finale, or Sibelius) for visualizing and practicing bass lines.
- A Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) like Ableton Live, Logic Pro, or FL Studio for recording and playback.
- A chord chart or lead sheet for the exercises (provided below).

## Exercise 1: Foundational Practical Implementation

### Exercise 1.1: Walking Bass Line Over a Simple Chord Progression

**Chord Progression:** Cmaj7 - Fmaj7 - G7 - Cmaj7

**Steps:**
Set the metronome to a moderate tempo of 120 BPM, ensuring it clicks on the quarter note. Use your music notation software to write out the chord symbols and bass clef staff, placing the chord symbols above the staff and leaving the bass notes blank for you to fill in. Construct the walking bass line as follows:

- **Cmaj7:** Start on the root note C (C2). Move to the third E (E2) on the next beat. On the third beat, move to the fifth G (G2). On the fourth beat, return to the root C (C2).
- **Fmaj7:** Move from C (C2) to the root F (F2). On the next beat, move to the third A (A2). On the third beat, move to the fifth C (C3). On the fourth beat, return to the root F (F2).
- **G7:** Move from F (F2) to the root G (G2). On the next beat, move to the third B (B2). On the third beat, move to the fifth D (D3). On the fourth beat, return to the root G (G2).
- **Cmaj7:** Move from G (G2) to the root C (C2). On the next beat, move to the third E (E2). On the third beat, move to the fifth G (G2). On the fourth beat, return to the root C (C2).

Practice the walking bass line along with the metronome, focusing on smooth transitions between notes and maintaining a steady rhythm. Record your performance using your DAW to assess your timing and note accuracy.

## Exercise 2: Parameter Variation & Empirical Analysis

### Exercise 2.1: Varying the Rhythm of the Walking Bass Line

**Chord Progression:** Cmaj7 - Fmaj7 - G7 - Cmaj7

**Steps:**
Set the metronome to 120 BPM. Write out the chord symbols and bass clef staff for the progression. Construct the walking bass line with different rhythms:

- **Syncopated Rhythm:** Instead of playing quarter notes, try syncopating the rhythm. For example, play the root on the first beat, the third on the second beat, the fifth on the third beat, and the root on the fourth beat, but shift the emphasis to the off-beats.
- **Swing Rhythm:** Apply a swing feel to the walking bass line. Swing the eighth notes by playing the first eighth note slightly longer and the second eighth note slightly shorter.

Practice the walking bass line with the new rhythms along with the metronome, focusing on maintaining a steady tempo and smooth transitions between notes. Record your performance using your DAW to assess your timing and note accuracy.

## Exercise 3: Fault Injection & Stress Testing

### Exercise 3.1: Inducing Timing Errors

**Chord Progression:** Cmaj7 - Fmaj7 - G7 - Cmaj7

**Steps:**
Set the metronome to 120 BPM. Write out the chord symbols and bass clef staff for the progression. Induce timing errors:

- **Late Notes:** Intentionally play some notes late to simulate a timing error. For example, play the root of Cmaj7 on the second beat instead of the first beat.
- **Early Notes:** Intentionally play some notes early to simulate a timing error. For example, play the root of Cmaj7 on the fourth beat of the previous measure instead of the first beat of the current measure.

Practice the walking bass line with induced timing errors along with the metronome, focusing on maintaining a steady tempo and smooth transitions between notes. Record your performance using your DAW to assess your timing and note accuracy.
