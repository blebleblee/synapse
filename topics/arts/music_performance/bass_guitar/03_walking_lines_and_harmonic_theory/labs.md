# Operational Verification Labs: 03 Walking Lines And Harmonic Theory

## Objective & Lab Setup

### Objective
The objective of this laboratory is to deepen your understanding of walking bass lines and their harmonic underpinnings. Through hands-on exercises, you will learn to construct walking bass lines that effectively navigate chord progressions, emphasizing voice leading and harmonic tension resolution. This lab will also reinforce your knowledge of harmonic theory, including chord functions, voice leading rules, and modal interchange.

### Lab Setup
For this lab, you will need the following tools and environments:
- A bass guitar (electric or acoustic) or a digital audio workstation (DAW) with a bass guitar sample library.
- A metronome or drum machine.
- A music notation software (such as MuseScore, Finale, or Sibelius) to create and analyze walking bass lines.
- A basic understanding of music theory, including chord progressions and voice leading principles.

## Exercise 1: Foundational Practical Implementation

### Exercise 1.1: Walking Bass Line Over a Simple Progression
In this exercise, you will create a walking bass line over a simple chord progression in the key of C major. The progression is: Cmaj7 - Fmaj7 - G7 - Cmaj7.

#### Steps:
1. **Set Up the Metronome:**
   - Set your metronome to a moderate tempo (e.g., 120 BPM).
   - Ensure the metronome is set to click on every quarter note.

2. **Create the Chord Progression:**
   - Open your music notation software and create a new score.
   - Enter the chord symbols Cmaj7, Fmaj7, G7, and Cmaj7 in the bass clef, each lasting one measure.

3. **Construct the Walking Bass Line:**
   - Start with the root of the Cmaj7 chord (C) on the first beat.
   - Move to the third of the Cmaj7 chord (E) on the second beat.
   - Move to the fifth of the Cmaj7 chord (G) on the third beat.
   - Move to the root of the Fmaj7 chord (F) on the fourth beat.
   - Continue this process for the remaining chords, ensuring each note is played on a quarter note.
   - Use chromatic approach notes and passing tones to create a smooth line. For example, from G to F, you could use G, F# (chromatic approach), F.

4. **Play the Walking Bass Line:**
   - Practice playing the walking bass line along with the metronome.
   - Focus on maintaining a steady rhythm and clear articulation of each note.

## Exercise 2: Parameter Variation & Empirical Analysis

### Exercise 2.1: Varying the Tempo
In this exercise, you will analyze how varying the tempo affects the feel and execution of the walking bass line.

#### Steps:
1. **Set Up the Metronome:**
   - Start with a moderate tempo (e.g., 120 BPM).
   - Gradually increase the tempo by 10 BPM increments (130 BPM, 140 BPM, etc.).
   - Gradually decrease the tempo by 10 BPM increments (110 BPM, 100 BPM, etc.).

2. **Play the Walking Bass Line:**
   - Play the walking bass line at each tempo.
   - Observe how the feel of the line changes with each tempo.
   - Note any difficulties in maintaining a steady rhythm and clear articulation at faster or slower tempos.

### Exercise 2.2: Varying the Chord Progression
In this exercise, you will analyze how varying the chord progression affects the harmonic content and voice leading of the walking bass line.

#### Steps:
1. **Create New Chord Progressions:**
   - Create a new chord progression in the key of C major, such as Cmaj7 - Am7 - Fmaj7 - G7.
   - Create another chord progression in a different key, such as G major: Gmaj7 - Em7 - Cmaj7 - D7.

2. **Construct the Walking Bass Lines:**
   - Construct walking bass lines for each new chord progression.
   - Use chromatic approach notes and passing tones to create smooth lines.

3. **Play the Walking Bass Lines:**
   - Play the walking bass lines for each chord progression.
   - Observe how the harmonic content and voice leading change with each progression.
   - Note any difficulties in navigating the chord changes and maintaining a smooth line.

## Exercise 3: Fault Injection & Stress Testing

### Exercise 3.1: Introducing Chromatic Approach Notes
In this exercise, you will introduce chromatic approach notes to create tension and resolution in the walking bass line.

#### Steps:
1. **Create the Chord Progression:**
   - Use the chord progression from Exercise 1.1: Cmaj7 - Fmaj7 - G7 - Cmaj7.

2. **Introduce Chromatic Approach Notes:**
   - Introduce chromatic approach notes between the root and third of each chord.
   - For example, from C to E in the Cmaj7 chord, use C, C#, D, E.

3. **Play the Walking Bass Line:**
   - Play the walking bass line with chromatic approach notes.
   - Observe how the chromatic approach notes create tension and resolution.

2. **Introduce Passing Tones:**
   - Introduce passing tones between the third and fifth of each chord.

3. **Play the Walking Bass Line:**

   - Observe how the passing tones create a more fluid and dynamic line.

## Practical Cheat Sheet & Quick Reference

### Essential Commands and Parameters
- **Metronome Settings:**
  - Tempo: 120 BPM (adjustable in 10 BPM increments)
  - Click on every quarter note

- **Chord Progressions:**
  - Simple progression: Cmaj7 - Fmaj7 - G7 - Cmaj7
  - Complex progression: Cmaj7 - D7 - G7 - Cmaj7
  - Key change progression: Gmaj7 - Em7 - Cmaj7 - D7

- **Walking Bass Line Construction:**
  - Start with the root of the first chord.
  - Move to the third, then the fifth of the chord.
  - Move to the root of the next chord.
  - Use chromatic approach notes and passing tones to create a smooth line.

- **Harmonic Theory:**
  - Chord functions: Tonic (I), Subdominant (IV), Dominant (V)
  - Voice leading rules: Avoid parallel fifths and octaves, use consonant and dissonant suspensions.
  - Modal interchange: Borrow chords from parallel keys to create harmonic tension and resolution.

- **Verification Checks:**
  - Ensure steady rhythm and clear articulation of each note.
  - Observe how varying the tempo and chord progression affects the feel and execution of the walking bass line.
  - Introduce chromatic approach notes and passing tones to create tension and resolution.
