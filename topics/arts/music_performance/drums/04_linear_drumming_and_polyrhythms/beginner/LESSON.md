# Beginner Technical Lesson: Drums 04 Linear Drumming And Polyrhythms
## 1. Core Mechanics & Basic Principles
**Linear Drumming** involves playing a steady, rhythmic pattern that moves in a linear fashion, typically from one drum to another in a sequence. This technique is foundational for developing coordination and control over your drumming.

**Polyrhythms** are rhythms that consist of two or more conflicting rhythms played simultaneously. They add complexity and depth to your playing and are a key element in many styles of music, including jazz, funk, and world music.

Understanding these concepts requires a solid grasp of basic drumming techniques and the ability to count and feel different rhythmic patterns.

## 2. Step-by-Step Component Breakdown
### Linear Drumming
1. **Start with a Simple Pattern:** Begin with a basic linear pattern using the hi-hat, snare, and bass drum. A common pattern is the "ride" pattern: hi-hat on every beat, snare on beats 2 and 4, and bass drum on beat 1.
2. **Practice with a Metronome:** Use a metronome to keep a steady tempo. Start slow and gradually increase the speed as you become more comfortable.
3. **Move to Different Sticks:** Practice using different sticks (e.g., brushes, mallets) to add variety and control to your linear patterns.

### Polyrhythms
1. **Understand the Concept:** A simple example of a polyrhythm is 3 over 2. This means playing a pattern of three notes in the same time it takes to play two notes of a different pattern.
2. **Start with Simple Patterns:** Begin with a simple 3 over 2 pattern. For example, play the hi-hat on every third beat (1, 4, 7) while playing the bass drum on every second beat (1, 3, 5, 7).
3. **Practice with a Metronome:** Use a metronome to keep a steady tempo. Start slow and gradually increase the speed as you become more comfortable.

## 3. First Working Implementation
Here is a simple implementation of a linear drumming pattern followed by a 3 over 2 polyrhythm pattern. We will use a basic notation where:
- `H` represents the hi-hat
- `S` represents the snare
- `B` represents the bass drum

**Linear Drumming Pattern (Ride Pattern):**
```
H S H H S H H S H H S H
```

**3 Over 2 Polyrhythm Pattern:**
```
H   H   H   H
B     B     B
```

**Combined Pattern:**
```
H   H   H   H
B S H B S H B
```

## 4. What Can Go Wrong (Simple Common Mistakes and How to Spot Them)
### Linear Drumming
- **Inconsistent Tempo:** Ensure you are keeping a steady tempo with the metronome. Listen carefully and adjust your playing.
- **Uneven Strokes:** Practice even strokes on each drum surface. Use a mirror to check your technique.
- **Lack of Coordination:** Focus on coordinating your hands and feet. Start with simple patterns and gradually increase complexity.

### Polyrhythms
- **Confusion Between Patterns:** Clearly define each pattern in your mind before attempting to play them together. Practice each pattern separately before combining them.
- **Inconsistent Tempo:** Polyrhythms require a steady tempo. Use a metronome to keep the tempo consistent.
- **Overthinking:** Don't overthink the patterns. Focus on feeling the rhythm and playing with confidence.

## 5. Key Takeaways & Review Checklist
- **Linear Drumming:** Start with simple patterns, use a metronome, and practice with different sticks.
- **Polyrhythms:** Understand the concept, start with simple patterns, and use a metronome.
- **Common Mistakes:** Be aware of inconsistent tempo, uneven strokes, lack of coordination, and confusion between patterns.
- **Review Checklist:**
  - Can you play a simple linear drumming pattern with a metronome?
  - Can you play a 3 over 2 polyrhythm pattern with a metronome?
  - Can you combine a simple linear pattern with a 3 over 2 polyrhythm pattern?
  - Are you able to identify and correct common mistakes in your playing?
