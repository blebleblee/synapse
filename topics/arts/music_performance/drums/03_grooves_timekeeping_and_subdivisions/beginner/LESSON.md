# Beginner Technical Lesson: Drums 03 Grooves Timekeeping And Subdivisions
## 1. Core Mechanics & Basic Principles (How it works under the hood at a fundamental level)

### Grooves
A groove is a rhythmic pattern that provides a foundation for music. It is the heartbeat of a song, and it's what makes people want to dance or clap along. Grooves are typically played on drums and percussion instruments and are characterized by a consistent, repeating pattern.

### Timekeeping
Timekeeping in drumming is the ability to maintain a steady beat and rhythm throughout a piece of music. It involves keeping track of the tempo (speed of the beat) and the time signature (the number of beats per measure). Good timekeeping is crucial for playing in sync with other musicians and for creating a solid foundation for the music.

### Subdivisions
Subdivisions are smaller rhythmic units that fit within a larger beat. For example, in a 4/4 time signature, the basic beat is divided into four quarter notes. Subdivisions can be eighth notes, sixteenth notes, or even triplets, which fit within the space of a quarter note. Understanding and practicing subdivisions helps you play more complex rhythms and adds depth to your grooves.

## 2. Step-by-Step Component Breakdown

### Grooves
1. **Basic Beat**: Start with a simple beat, such as a rock groove, which typically consists of a bass drum on every quarter note, a snare drum on beats 2 and 4, and a hi-hat on every eighth note.
2. **Variations**: Experiment with different patterns by adding or removing elements. For example, you can play the hi-hat on every quarter note instead of every eighth note, or you can add a ride cymbal on beats 1 and 3.

### Timekeeping
1. **Metronome**: Use a metronome to practice keeping time. Start with a slow tempo and gradually increase the speed as you become more comfortable.
2. **Counting**: Count out loud to help you keep track of the beats. For example, in 4/4 time, count "1-2-3-4" or "1-and-2-and-3-and-4" for eighth notes.

### Subdivisions
1. **Eighth Notes**: Practice playing eighth notes by dividing each quarter note into two equal parts. Use the hi-hat to play eighth notes and the bass drum on quarter notes.
2. **Sixteenth Notes**: Divide each eighth note into two equal parts to play sixteenth notes. Use the snare drum on quarter notes, the bass drum on beats 2 and 4, and the hi-hat on sixteenth notes.
3. **Triplets**: Divide each quarter note into three equal parts to play triplets. Use the hi-hat for triplets and the bass drum on quarter notes.

## 4. What Can Go Wrong (Simple common mistakes and how to spot them)

### Grooves
- **Inconsistent Beat**: If the beat is not steady, it can disrupt the flow of the music. Listen for variations in the timing of the beats.
- **Lack of Variation**: Playing the same groove repeatedly can become monotonous. Experiment with different patterns to keep the music interesting.

### Timekeeping
- **Rushing or Dragging**: Rushing means playing too fast, while dragging means playing too slow. Use a metronome to help maintain a consistent tempo.
- **Counting Errors**: Miscounting can lead to mistakes in timing. Practice counting out loud to ensure accuracy.

### Subdivisions
- **Uneven Rhythms**: If the subdivisions are not evenly spaced, it can sound uneven and unprofessional. Practice slowly and gradually increase the speed.
- **Inconsistent Dynamics**: Varying the volume of the hits can disrupt the groove. Try to maintain a consistent dynamic level for each part of the groove.
