# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Organ
## Objective & Prerequisites
### Objective
The objective of this lab is to familiarize beginners with the basic setup and operation of an organ, whether it is a physical instrument or a digital emulation. This lab will guide you through setting up your environment, executing your first commands, and inspecting the basic state of the organ.

### Prerequisites
- Access to a physical organ or a digital organ emulator software (such as Hauptwerk, Aeolus, or any other software that simulates an organ).
- Basic understanding of musical notation and keyboard instruments.

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check (Exact commands to verify tools)
### Physical Organ
1. **Ensure the Organ is Ready:**
   - Make sure the organ is powered on and the console is accessible.
   - Check that all the stops (drawknobs) are in the off position.
   - Verify that the pedals are in the neutral position.

### Digital Organ Emulator
1. **Install the Software:**
   - Download and install your chosen digital organ emulator software.
   - Follow the installation instructions provided by the software manufacturer.

2. **Verify Installation:**
   - Launch the software and ensure it opens without errors.
   - Check that the software interface is responsive and that you can navigate through the settings.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment (Step-by-step guided instructions with expected outputs)
### Physical Organ
1. **Play a Simple Scale:**
   - Turn on the Great 8' stop by pulling the corresponding drawknob.
   - Play a C major scale (C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C) using the manuals (keyboards).
   - Expected Output: You should hear a clear, resonant sound for each note.

2. **Experiment with Pedals:**
   - Turn on the Pedal 16' stop.
   - Play the C major scale using the pedals.
   - Expected Output: You should hear a deep, powerful sound for each note.

### Digital Organ Emulator
1. **Load a Sample Organ:**
   - Open the software and load a sample organ from the library.
   - Ensure that the sample organ is selected and ready to play.

2. **Play a Simple Scale:**
   - Turn on the Great 8' stop by clicking the corresponding button in the software.
   - Play a C major scale (C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C) using the virtual manuals.
   - Expected Output: You should hear a clear, resonant sound for each note.

3. **Experiment with Pedals:**
   - Turn on the Pedal 16' stop.
   - Play the C major scale using the virtual pedals.
   - Expected Output: You should hear a deep, powerful sound for each note.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State (Reading logs or simple outputs)
### Physical Organ
1. **Check Stop Configuration:**
   - Look at the drawknobs to ensure that only the stops you turned on are engaged.
   - Verify that the manuals and pedals are in the correct position.

2. **Listen to the Sound:**
   - Play a few chords and listen to the sound produced.
   - Ensure that the sound is clear and that there are no unwanted noises.

### Digital Organ Emulator
1. **Check Stop Configuration:**
   - Look at the software interface to ensure that only the stops you turned on are engaged.
   - Verify that the manuals and pedals are in the correct position.

2. **Listen to the Sound:**
   - Play a few chords and listen to the sound produced.
   - Ensure that the sound is clear and that there are no unwanted noises.

## Challenge Exercise (Simple self-test for beginners)
### Physical Organ
1. **Play a Simple Chord Progression:**
   - Turn on the Great 8' and Swell 8' stops.
   - Play a simple chord progression (C, G, Am, F) using the manuals.
   - Expected Output: You should hear a harmonious progression of chords.

### Digital Organ Emulator
1. **Play a Simple Chord Progression:**
   - Turn on the Great 8' and Swell 8' stops.
   - Play a simple chord progression (C, G, Am, F) using the virtual manuals.
   - Expected Output: You should hear a harmonious progression of chords.

By completing this lab, you should have a basic understanding of how to set up and operate an organ, whether it is a physical instrument or a digital emulation. This foundation will help you as you continue to explore and develop your skills as an organist.
