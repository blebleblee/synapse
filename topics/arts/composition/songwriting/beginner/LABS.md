# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Songwriting
## Objective & Prerequisites

### Objective
The objective of this lab is to introduce you to the basic tools and environment necessary for songwriting. By the end of this lab, you will have set up a songwriting environment, created a simple musical piece, and inspected the basic state of your work.

### Prerequisites
- Basic familiarity with a computer and its operating system (Windows, macOS, or Linux).
- Access to a digital audio workstation (DAW) such as GarageBand, FL Studio, Ableton Live, or MuseScore.
- A willingness to follow step-by-step instructions and experiment with basic musical concepts.

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check (Exact commands to verify tools)

### Step 1: Install a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW)
If you do not already have a DAW installed, download and install one of the following:
- **GarageBand** (macOS)
- **FL Studio** (Windows, macOS)
- **Ableton Live** (Windows, macOS)
- **MuseScore** (Windows, macOS, Linux)

### Step 2: Verify Installation
Open the DAW application to ensure it launches correctly. Perform a simple sanity check by creating a new project or opening a sample project that comes with the DAW.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment (Step-by-step guided instructions with expected outputs)

### Step 1: Create a New Project
Open your DAW and create a new project. This is typically done through the "File" menu, selecting "New Project" or "Create New Project."

### Step 2: Add a Musical Instrument
Add a musical instrument to your project. This could be a software instrument or a sample library. For example, in GarageBand, you can add a piano or guitar track by clicking the "+" button and selecting an instrument.

### Step 3: Record or Input a Melody
- **Recording**: If your DAW supports recording, plug in a microphone or instrument and record a simple melody.
- **Input**: Alternatively, you can manually input notes using the piano roll or MIDI keyboard. For example, in FL Studio, you can use the piano roll to enter notes by clicking on the grid.

### Step 4: Save Your Work
Save your project to ensure that your work is not lost. This is typically done through the "File" menu, selecting "Save" or "Save As."

### Expected Output
You should have a new project with a track containing a simple melody. The project should be saved on your computer.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State (Reading logs or simple outputs)

### Step 1: Review Your Project
Open your saved project and review the track you created. Ensure that the melody is present and that the notes are correctly placed.

### Step 2: Play Back Your Melody
Play back the melody to hear how it sounds. Adjust the volume or instrument settings if necessary to achieve the desired sound.

### Step 3: Export Your Melody
Export your melody as an audio file to share or listen to outside of the DAW. This is typically done through the "File" menu, selecting "Export" or "Export As."

### Expected Output
You should be able to play back the melody and export it as an audio file.

## Challenge Exercise (Simple self-test for beginners)

### Challenge
Create a simple chord progression using the same DAW. Add a second track with a different instrument (e.g., bass or drums) and record or input a simple rhythm or bassline to accompany the chord progression.

### Expected Output
You should have a project with two tracks: one containing a chord progression and the other containing a rhythm or bassline. Play back the project to hear how the two tracks work together.

By completing this lab, you have successfully set up a songwriting environment, created a simple musical piece, and inspected the basic state of your work. Congratulations on taking your first steps in songwriting!
