# Beginner Technical Lesson: Songwriting
## 1. Core Mechanics & Basic Principles (How it works under the hood at a fundamental level)

Songwriting is the art of composing music with lyrics, melody, harmony, and rhythm. At its core, songwriting involves the interplay of these elements to convey emotion and meaning. Let's break down each component:

### Melody
Melody is the sequence of notes that forms a tune. It is the most memorable part of a song and often carries the lyrics. Melodies are constructed using scales, which are ordered sets of notes. The most common scales are the major and minor scales. For example, the C major scale consists of the notes C, D, E, F, G, A, and B.

### Harmony
Harmony involves the simultaneous sounding of notes. Chords are the building blocks of harmony. A chord is a group of three or more notes played together. Chords are typically built from the notes of a scale. For instance, in the key of C major, the C major chord (C-E-G) is constructed from the first, third, and fifth notes of the C major scale.

### Rhythm
Rhythm is the pattern of beats and silences in music. It is what gives music its pulse and drive. Rhythm is defined by time signatures, which indicate the number of beats per measure and the type of note that receives one beat. A common time signature is 4/4, which means there are four beats per measure and a quarter note receives one beat.

### Lyrics
Lyrics are the words that are sung in a song. They provide the narrative and emotional content of the song. Lyrics are often structured into verses, choruses, and bridges. The lyrics should complement the melody and harmony, enhancing the overall message of the song.

## 2. Step-by-Step Component Breakdown

### Step 1: Choose a Key and Scale
Decide on the key and scale for your song. This will determine the notes you can use for your melody and chords. For simplicity, let's choose the key of C major.

### Step 2: Develop a Melody
Create a simple melody using the notes of the C major scale. Start with a basic four-bar phrase. For example:
- C - E - G - C
- E - G - C - E
- G - B - D - G
- E - G - C - C

### Step 3: Create Chords
Choose chords that complement your melody. In the key of C major, common chords are C, G, Am, and F. Here is a simple chord progression:
- C - G - Am - F
- C - G - Am - F

### Step 4: Add Rhythm
Determine the rhythm of your melody and chords. In 4/4 time, you can use quarter notes, eighth notes, and half notes. For example, you might use quarter notes for the chords and eighth notes for the melody.

### Step 5: Write Lyrics
Write lyrics that fit the rhythm and melody of your song. Ensure the lyrics convey a clear message and emotion. For example:

  The world is bright, and everything is mine  
  I take a deep breath, and I start the day  
  With hope and joy, I'll make my way

- **Chorus:**  
  Oh, I'm living my life, living my dream  
  I won't stop until I reach the top  
  I'm living my life, living my dream  
  I won't stop until I reach the top

## 4. What Can Go Wrong (Simple common mistakes and how to spot them)

### Common Mistakes in Songwriting
1. **Lack of Structure:** Ensure your song has a clear structure with verses, choruses, and bridges. Without structure, your song may feel disjointed.
2. **Repetitive Melodies:** Avoid using the same melody for every section of your song. Vary the melody in the verses, choruses, and bridges to keep the listener engaged.
3. **Poor Chord Progressions:** Choose chords that complement your melody. Avoid using chords that clash with the melody or create dissonance.
4. **Weak Lyrics:** Ensure your lyrics are clear and convey a message. Avoid using vague or cliché lyrics that do not add to the song.
5. **Inconsistent Rhythm:** Maintain a consistent rhythm throughout your song. Inconsistent rhythm can make your song feel chaotic and difficult to follow.

### How to Spot Common Mistakes
- **Listen to Your Song:** Play your song and listen to it carefully. Does it flow smoothly? Does it have a clear structure?
- **Check Your Melody:** Compare your melody to the chords. Do the notes of the melody fit well with the chords? Are there any dissonant notes?
- **Review Your Lyrics:** Read your lyrics out loud. Do they make sense? Do they convey a clear message?
- **Seek Feedback:** Share your song with others and ask for feedback. Are there any sections that need improvement?
