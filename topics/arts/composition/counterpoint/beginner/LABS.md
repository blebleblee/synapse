# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Counterpoint
## Objective & Prerequisites

### Objective
The objective of this hands-on lab is to introduce you to the fundamental concepts of counterpoint through practical exercises. By the end of this lab, you will have set up a basic environment for working with counterpoint, created your first simple counterpoint exercise, and inspected the results to ensure understanding.

### Prerequisites
- Basic knowledge of music theory, including scales, intervals, and chords.
- Access to a music notation software such as MuseScore, Finale, or Sibelius. For this lab, we will use MuseScore, which is free and open-source.
- Basic familiarity with using a computer and navigating file systems.

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check (Exact commands to verify tools)

### Step 1: Download and Install MuseScore
1. Visit the official MuseScore website: [MuseScore Download](https://musescore.org/en/download).
2. Download the appropriate version for your operating system (Windows, macOS, or Linux).
3. Follow the installation instructions provided on the website.

### Step 2: Verify Installation
1. Open MuseScore from your applications menu or desktop shortcut.
2. You should see the MuseScore interface with a new, untitled score open by default.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment (Step-by-step guided instructions with expected outputs)

### Step 1: Create a New Score
1. Open MuseScore.
2. Click on "File" in the top menu, then select "New Score."
3. In the "Create New Score" dialog, choose "Classical" as the style and "Two Voices" as the form.
4. Click "OK" to create the score.

### Step 2: Enter the Cantus Firmus
1. Click on the first measure of the top staff (Voice 1).
2. Enter the following notes using the piano keyboard or the note entry palette:
   - C4 (whole note)
   - E4 (whole note)
   - G4 (whole note)
   - C5 (whole note)

### Step 3: Enter the Counterpoint
1. Click on the first measure of the bottom staff (Voice 2).
2. Enter the following notes using the piano keyboard or the note entry palette:
   - E4 (whole note)
   - G4 (whole note)
   - C5 (whole note)
   - E5 (whole note)

### Step 4: Save Your Work
1. Click on "File" in the top menu, then select "Save As."
2. Choose a location to save your file, name it "FirstCounterpoint.mscz," and click "Save."

### Expected Output
You should have a simple two-voice score with a cantus firmus in the top staff and a counterpoint in the bottom staff. The notes should be whole notes, and the pitches should match the sequence provided.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State (Reading logs or simple outputs)

### Step 1: Play Back the Score
1. Click on the play button (triangle icon) in the toolbar to play back the score.
2. Listen to the playback to ensure that the cantus firmus and counterpoint are in harmony.

### Step 2: Check for Errors
1. Click on "Tools" in the top menu, then select "Check for Errors."
2. MuseScore will analyze the score for common errors such as parallel fifths or octaves.
3. If there are any errors, they will be highlighted in the score. For this simple example, there should be no errors.

## Challenge Exercise (Simple self-test for beginners)

### Task
Modify the counterpoint in the bottom staff to create a simple first species counterpoint. Ensure that the counterpoint follows these rules:
- Use only whole notes.
- Avoid parallel fifths and octaves.
- Use consonant intervals (unisons, thirds, fifths, sixths, and octaves).

### Steps
1. Click on the first measure of the bottom staff.
2. Change the E4 to a D4.
3. Click on the second measure of the bottom staff.
4. Change the G4 to an F4.
5. Click on the third measure of the bottom staff.
6. Change the C5 to a B4.
7. Click on the fourth measure of the bottom staff.
8. Change the E5 to a D5.

### Expected Output
You should have a simple first species counterpoint in the bottom staff that follows the rules provided. Play back the score to ensure that the counterpoint is in harmony with the cantus firmus and that there are no parallel fifths or octaves.

By completing this lab, you have successfully set up your environment for working with counterpoint, created your first simple counterpoint exercise, and inspected the results to ensure understanding. Well done!
