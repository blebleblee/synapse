# Advanced Operational Lab: Counterpoint
## Objective & Deep Prerequisites

### Objective
The objective of this advanced operational lab is to deepen your understanding of counterpoint through a series of exercises that involve low-level state inspection, advanced scenario and performance stress testing, intentional fault injection, and root-cause crash analysis. This lab will challenge you to apply your knowledge of counterpoint in a rigorous and diagnostic manner, akin to debugging complex software systems.

### Deep Prerequisites
- Proficiency in Fuxian species counterpoint, including cantus firmus rules, parallel fifth/octave prohibitions, consonant vs. dissonant suspensions, and invertible counterpoint.
- Familiarity with Roman numeral analysis, secondary dominants, tritone substitutions, modal interchange, and neo-Riemannian voice leading.
- Basic understanding of digital signal processing (DSP) concepts, including discrete-time sampling, Nyquist frequencies, quantization noise, and the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT / FFT).
- Proficiency in using music notation software such as Sibelius, Finale, or MuseScore.
- Basic knowledge of debugging tools and techniques, including the use of debuggers and memory dumps.

## Exercise 1: Low-Level State Inspection (Hardware/kernel registers, debuggers, memory dumps)

### Purpose
To understand the internal state of a counterpoint composition at various stages of development, akin to inspecting hardware/kernel registers and memory dumps in software debugging.

### Procedure
1. **Select a Cantus Firmus**: Choose a cantus firmus from a well-known source, such as Fux's *Gradus ad Parnassum*.
2. **Create a Memory Dump**: Write out the cantus firmus and its harmonic context in a detailed score format, including all relevant harmonic information (e.g., Roman numerals, key signatures).
3. **Use a Debugger**: Utilize a music notation software debugger or a similar tool to inspect the internal state of your composition. This could involve checking the harmonic progression, voice leading, and adherence to counterpoint rules.
4. **Analyze the State**: Identify any discrepancies or issues in the composition, such as parallel fifths or octaves, forbidden dissonances, or improper voice leading.

### Deliverables
- A detailed score of the cantus firmus and its harmonic context.
- A report on the internal state inspection, including any identified issues and their root causes.

## Exercise 2: Advanced Scenario & Performance Stress Testing

### Purpose
To test the robustness and performance of a counterpoint composition under various scenarios, similar to stress testing in software engineering.

### Procedure
1. **Create a Complex Counterpoint**: Write a complex counterpoint using the cantus firmus from Exercise 1, incorporating multiple voices and complex harmonic progressions.
2. **Define Scenarios**: Create a set of scenarios that test the composition's adherence to counterpoint rules under different conditions. For example, test the composition with different key signatures, modes, or harmonic progressions.
3. **Perform Stress Testing**: Use a music notation software tool to perform stress testing on the composition. This could involve checking the composition's performance under different scenarios and identifying any issues that arise.
4. **Analyze Performance**: Evaluate the composition's performance under each scenario, identifying any issues or discrepancies.

### Deliverables
- A detailed score of the complex counterpoint.
- A report on the stress testing results, including any identified issues and their root causes.

## Exercise 3: Intentional Fault Injection & Root-Cause Crash Analysis

### Purpose
To understand the effects of intentional faults in a counterpoint composition and to perform root-cause crash analysis, similar to fault injection and crash analysis in software engineering.

### Procedure
1. **Introduce Faults**: Introduce intentional faults into the counterpoint composition from Exercise 2. For example, introduce parallel fifths or octaves, forbidden dissonances, or improper voice leading.
2. **Perform Fault Injection**: Use a music notation software tool to perform fault injection on the composition. This could involve modifying the composition to introduce the faults and checking the results.
3. **Analyze Faults**: Evaluate the effects of the faults on the composition, identifying any issues or discrepancies.
4. **Perform Root-Cause Analysis**: Perform root-cause analysis to identify the underlying causes of the faults. This could involve checking the composition's adherence to counterpoint rules and identifying any issues in the harmonic progression, voice leading, or other aspects of the composition.

### Deliverables
- A detailed score of the counterpoint composition with intentional faults.
- A report on the fault injection results, including any identified issues and their root causes.

## Master Challenge Exercise (Research-grade problem)

### Purpose
To tackle a research-grade problem in counterpoint, similar to solving complex software engineering problems.

### Procedure
1. **Select a Research Problem**: Choose a research problem in counterpoint, such as the development of a new counterpoint rule or the analysis of a complex counterpoint composition.
2. **Perform Research**: Conduct research on the selected problem, including a literature review and analysis of existing counterpoint compositions.
3. **Develop a Solution**: Develop a solution to the research problem, incorporating your knowledge of counterpoint and your findings from the research.
4. **Evaluate the Solution**: Evaluate the solution, checking its adherence to counterpoint rules and its effectiveness in solving the research problem.

### Deliverables
- A detailed report on the research problem, including a literature review and analysis of existing counterpoint compositions.
- A detailed score of the solution, including any new counterpoint rules or complex counterpoint compositions.
- A report on the evaluation of the solution, including any identified issues and their root causes.
