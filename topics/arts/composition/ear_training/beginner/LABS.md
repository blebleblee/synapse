# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Ear Training
## Objective & Prerequisites
The objective of this lab is to introduce you to the foundational skills of ear training, which are essential for any composer or musician. This lab will guide you through setting up a basic environment for ear training, executing your first ear training exercises, and verifying your setup. By the end of this lab, you should be able to confidently identify basic musical intervals and rhythms.

### Prerequisites
- A computer with audio output capabilities.
- A pair of headphones or speakers.
- A basic understanding of music theory, including intervals and rhythms.
- A willingness to listen and practice attentively.

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check (Exact commands to verify tools)
For this lab, we will use a simple audio player and a metronome. You can use any audio player and metronome application available on your computer. For the sake of consistency, we will use `VLC` for audio playback and `Metronome` for rhythm training. Ensure these tools are installed on your system.

### VLC Media Player
1. **Download VLC Media Player:**
   - Visit the official VLC website: [VLC Downloads](https://www.videolan.org/vlc/download-windows.html) (or the appropriate page for your operating system).
   - Follow the installation instructions for your operating system.

2. **Verify VLC Installation:**
   - Open VLC Media Player from your applications menu.
   - Play a sample audio file to ensure it is working correctly.

### Metronome
1. **Download Metronome:**
   - For Windows, you can use the built-in Metronome app.
   - For macOS, you can use the built-in Metronome app.
   - Alternatively, you can download a free metronome app from the App Store (macOS) or Google Play Store (Windows).

2. **Verify Metronome Installation:**
   - Open the Metronome app.
   - Set the tempo to 120 BPM and start the metronome to ensure it is working correctly.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment (Step-by-step guided instructions with expected outputs)
In this exercise, we will practice identifying basic musical intervals using a sample audio file. We will use a pre-recorded audio file containing a series of intervals.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Download the Sample Audio File:**
   - Visit the following link to download the sample audio file: [Sample Intervals](https://example.com/sample_intervals.mp3).
   - Save the file to a known location on your computer.

2. **Open VLC Media Player:**
   - Launch VLC Media Player from your applications menu.

3. **Play the Sample Audio File:**
   - In VLC, go to `Media` > `Open File...`.
   - Navigate to the location where you saved the sample audio file and select it.
   - Click `Open` to play the audio file.

4. **Identify the Intervals:**
   - Listen carefully to each pair of notes played in the sample audio file.
   - Try to identify the interval between the two notes. Common intervals to listen for include:
     - **Unison (0 semitones)**
     - **Minor Second (1 semitone)**
     - **Major Second (2 semitones)**
     - **Minor Third (3 semitones)**
     - **Major Third (4 semitones)**
     - **Perfect Fourth (5 semitones)**
     - **Perfect Fifth (7 semitones)**
     - **Minor Sixth (8 semitones)**
     - **Major Sixth (9 semitones)**
     - **Minor Seventh (10 semitones)**
     - **Major Seventh (11 semitones)**
     - **Octave (12 semitones)**

### Expected Outputs
- You should be able to identify the intervals played in the sample audio file. The intervals in the sample file are as follows (in order):
  1. Unison
  2. Minor Second
  3. Major Second
  4. Minor Third
  5. Major Third
  6. Perfect Fourth
  7. Perfect Fifth
  8. Minor Sixth
  9. Major Sixth
  10. Minor Seventh
  11. Major Seventh
  12. Octave

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State (Reading logs or simple outputs)
In this exercise, we will practice keeping time using a metronome. This will help you develop a sense of rhythm and timing, which is crucial for ear training.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Open the Metronome App:**
   - Launch the Metronome app from your applications menu.

2. **Set the Tempo:**
   - Set the tempo to 120 BPM (Beats Per Minute).

3. **Start the Metronome:**
   - Start the metronome and begin tapping your foot or clapping along with the beat.
   - Try to maintain a steady rhythm and match the tempo of the metronome.

4. **Adjust the Tempo:**
   - Gradually increase the tempo to 140 BPM and repeat the exercise.
   - Gradually decrease the tempo to 100 BPM and repeat the exercise.

### Expected Outputs
- You should be able to maintain a steady rhythm and match the tempo of the metronome at different speeds. This will help you develop a sense of internal timing and rhythm.

## Challenge Exercise (Simple self-test for beginners)
In this challenge exercise, you will practice identifying intervals and keeping time simultaneously. This will test your ability to focus on both pitch and rhythm.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Download the Sample Audio File:**
   - Visit the following link to download the sample audio file: [Sample Intervals with Rhythm](https://example.com/sample_intervals_with_rhythm.mp3).
   - Save the file to a known location on your computer.

2. **Open VLC Media Player:**
   - Launch VLC Media Player from your applications menu.

3. **Play the Sample Audio File:**
   - In VLC, go to `Media` > `Open File...`.
   - Navigate to the location where you saved the sample audio file and select it.
   - Click `Open` to play the audio file.

4. **Identify the Intervals and Keep Time:**
   - Listen carefully to each pair of notes played in the sample audio file.
   - Try to identify the interval between the two notes.
   - Simultaneously, tap your foot or clap along with the beat of the metronome embedded in the audio file.

### Expected Outputs
- You should be able to identify the intervals played in the sample audio file while maintaining a steady rhythm. The intervals in the sample file are the same as in Exercise 2, but with a metronome beat embedded in the audio file.

By completing this lab, you should have a better understanding of basic ear training techniques and the importance of developing your sense of pitch and rhythm. Keep practicing these exercises regularly to improve your ear training skills.
