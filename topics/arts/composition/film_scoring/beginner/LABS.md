# Beginner Hands-On Lab: Film Scoring

## Objective & Prerequisites

### Objective
The objective of this hands-on lab is to familiarize beginners with the essential tools and environment setup required for film scoring. By the end of this lab, you will have set up a basic digital audio workstation (DAW) and created a simple musical piece that mimics a film score.

### Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of music theory (scales, chords, and simple melodies).
- Access to a computer with internet connectivity.
- A DAW software installed (e.g., FL Studio, Ableton Live, GarageBand, or Logic Pro). For this lab, we will use **FL Studio** as the DAW.
- Basic familiarity with your computer's operating system (Windows, macOS, or Linux).

## Exercise 1: Environment Setup & Sanity Check

### Step 1: Install FL Studio
If you haven't already installed FL Studio, download and install it from the official website: [FL Studio Download](https://www.image-line.com/flstudio/).

### Step 2: Launch FL Studio
Open FL Studio on your computer. You should see the main interface with various panels and tools.

### Step 3: Verify Installation
To ensure that FL Studio is installed correctly, perform a simple sanity check:
- Click on `File` > `New` to create a new project.
- Click on `Add Channel` > `Add Instrument Channel` to add a new instrument track.
- Click on the instrument track to select it, and then click on the `Piano Roll` button to open the piano roll editor.
- In the piano roll editor, click on the `Pattern` dropdown menu and select `New Pattern`.
- Click on the `Pattern` dropdown menu again and select `Play Pattern`. You should hear a default instrument sound playing a simple pattern.

## Exercise 2: Your First Working Experiment

### Step 1: Create a New Project
- Click on `File` > `New` to create a new project.

### Step 2: Add an Instrument Track
- Click on `Add Channel` > `Add Instrument Channel` to add a new instrument track.
- Rename the track to `Main Theme`.

### Step 3: Select an Instrument
- Click on the instrument track to select it.
- Click on the instrument selector (the icon with a musical note) and choose an orchestral instrument, such as `Strings`.

### Step 4: Create a Simple Melody
- Click on the `Piano Roll` button to open the piano roll editor.
- Click on the `Pattern` dropdown menu and select `New Pattern`.
- In the piano roll editor, click on the grid to add notes. Create a simple melody using the following notes:
  - C4 (middle C) for 1 beat
  - E4 (E above middle C) for 1 beat
  - G4 (G above middle C) for 1 beat
  - C5 (C one octave above middle C) for 1 beat
  - G4 for 1 beat
  - E4 for 1 beat
  - C4 for 1 beat

### Step 5: Play the Melody
- Click on the `Pattern` dropdown menu and select `Play Pattern`. You should hear the simple melody you created.

## Exercise 3: Inspecting Basic State

### Step 1: View the Project Settings
- Click on `File` > `Project Settings` to view the project settings.
- Ensure that the project settings are set to the default values:
  - Sample Rate: 44100 Hz
  - Bit Depth: 16-bit
  - Tempo: 120 BPM

### Step 2: View the Channel Settings
- Click on the `Main Theme` track to select it.
- Click on the `Channel Settings` button (the icon with a gear) to view the channel settings.
- Ensure that the channel settings are set to the default values:
  - Instrument: Strings
  - Volume: 100%
  - Pan: Center

## Challenge Exercise

### Objective
Create a simple chord progression using the `Main Theme` track.

### Instructions
- In the piano roll editor, add the following chords:
  - C major chord (C4, E4, G4) for 2 beats
  - G major chord (G4, B4, D5) for 2 beats
  - Am minor chord (A4, C5, E5) for 2 beats
  - F major chord (F4, A4, C5) for 2 beats
- Play the chord progression and ensure that it sounds harmonious.

### Expected Output
You should hear a simple chord progression that mimics the beginning of a film score. The chords should progress smoothly from C major to G major, then to Am minor, and finally to F major.

By completing this hands-on lab, you have successfully set up your environment for film scoring and created a simple musical piece. Congratulations on taking your first steps in composing for film!
