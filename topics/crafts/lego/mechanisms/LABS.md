# Hands-On Operational Lab: Mechanisms
## Objective & Lab Prerequisites

### Objective
This lab will guide you through the process of building, configuring, and deploying a complex mechanism using LEGO. You will learn how to explore, diagnose, and debug issues in your LEGO mechanisms. By the end of this lab, you will have a solid understanding of how to create and troubleshoot mechanical systems using LEGO.

### Prerequisites
- A set of LEGO Technic pieces (e.g., LEGO Technic Set 42055)
- A LEGO Digital Designer (LDD) software or an online LEGO building platform
- Basic understanding of mechanical principles (gears, levers, pulleys)
- Access to a computer for design and simulation

## Exercise 1: Exploration, Diagnostics & System State Inspection

### Step 1: Design a Simple Mechanism
1. Open LEGO Digital Designer (LDD) or an online LEGO building platform.
2. Design a simple mechanism, such as a gear train or a lever system.

### Step 2: Simulate the Mechanism
1. Use the simulation feature in LDD to test your mechanism.
2. Observe the behavior of the mechanism and note any issues.

### Step 3: Inspect the System State
1. Take screenshots of the mechanism in different states.
2. Document any discrepancies or unexpected behavior.

### Expected Command Outputs and State Inspection
- **Command Output:** None (LDD is a graphical application)
- **State Inspection:** Document the behavior of the mechanism in the simulation. Look for smooth operation, proper gear engagement, and no wobbling or excessive friction.

## Exercise 2: Building, Configuring & Deploying a Production Scenario

### Step 1: Build the Mechanism
1. Follow your design from Exercise 1 to build the mechanism in real life.
2. Use precise measurements and tolerances to ensure accuracy.

### Step 2: Configure the Mechanism
1. Adjust gears, levers, and other components to ensure optimal performance.
2. Test the mechanism manually to ensure it operates smoothly.

### Step 3: Deploy the Mechanism
1. Place the mechanism in a functional environment (e.g., a LEGO city or a standalone display).
2. Observe the mechanism in action and make any necessary adjustments.

### Expected Command Outputs and State Inspection
- **Command Output:** None (physical construction)
- **State Inspection:** Document the behavior of the mechanism in real life. Ensure it operates as expected with no issues.

## Exercise 3: Fault Injection, Failure Analysis & Debugging

### Step 1: Introduce a Fault
1. Introduce a fault in your mechanism, such as a misaligned gear or a loose connection.
2. Observe how the fault affects the mechanism's performance.

### Step 2: Diagnose the Fault
1. Use the simulation in LDD to diagnose the fault.
2. Identify the root cause of the issue.

### Step 3: Fix the Fault
1. Correct the fault in your design.
2. Rebuild the mechanism and test it again.

### Expected Command Outputs and State Inspection
- **Command Output:** None (LDD is a graphical application)
- **State Inspection:** Document the behavior of the mechanism before and after fixing the fault. Ensure the mechanism operates smoothly after the correction.

## Challenge Lab: Independent Advanced Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Complex Gear Train
1. Design and build a complex gear train with multiple gears and shafts.
2. Simulate and test the mechanism to ensure it operates smoothly.
3. Document your design, simulation results, and real-world performance.

### Scenario 2: Automated Vehicle
1. Design and build an automated vehicle using LEGO Technic pieces.
2. Include features such as steering, propulsion, and braking mechanisms.
3. Simulate and test the vehicle to ensure it operates smoothly.
4. Document your design, simulation results, and real-world performance.

### Scenario 3: Robotic Arm
1. Design and build a robotic arm with multiple joints and a gripper.
2. Include features such as motorized joints and a control system.
3. Simulate and test the robotic arm to ensure it operates smoothly.
4. Document your design, simulation results, and real-world performance.
