# Hands-On Operational Lab: Investing in LEGO
## Objective & Lab Prerequisites

### Objective
This lab aims to guide you through the process of "investing" in LEGO by building a complex model, diagnosing issues, and debugging failures. You will learn how to plan, build, and troubleshoot LEGO models systematically.

### Prerequisites
- A set of LEGO bricks (preferably a large set like the LEGO Creator Expert series).
- A flat, stable workspace.
- A ruler or measuring tape.
- A LEGO instruction manual (if available).

## Exercise 1: Exploration, Diagnostics & System State Inspection

### Step 1: Planning and Inspection
1. **Inspect the Parts List:**
   - Open the LEGO instruction manual and review the parts list.
   - Ensure you have all the required parts by cross-referencing with your LEGO set.

2. **Measure the Workspace:**
   - Use a ruler or measuring tape to measure the dimensions of your workspace.
   - Ensure there is enough space to lay out and build your LEGO model.

### Expected Output
- A verified parts list.
- Workspace dimensions noted down.

### State Inspection
- Verify that all parts are present and accounted for.
- Confirm that the workspace is suitable for building the model.

## Exercise 2: Building, Configuring & Deploying a Production Scenario

### Step 1: Building the Model
1. **Follow the Instructions:**
   - Start building the LEGO model following the instructions step-by-step.
   - Pay close attention to the diagrams and part numbers.

2. **Check Structural Integrity:**
   - After completing each section, check the structural integrity by gently pressing on the parts to ensure they are securely connected.

### Expected Output
- A fully assembled LEGO model.
- Structural integrity verified at each step.

### State Inspection
- Ensure all parts are correctly placed and connected.
- Verify that the model stands upright and is stable.

## Exercise 3: Fault Injection, Failure Analysis & Debugging

### Step 1: Introduce a Fault
1. **Remove a Key Part:**
   - Deliberately remove a key part from the model (e.g., a beam or connector).

2. **Observe the Failure:**
   - Observe how the model behaves after removing the part.
   - Note any changes in stability or structure.

### Step 2: Diagnose and Fix the Fault
1. **Identify the Fault:**
   - Determine which part was removed and its function.
   - Understand how the removal of this part affected the model.

2. **Reinsert the Part:**
   - Reinsert the removed part back into its original position.
   - Check the model's stability and structure again.

### Expected Output
- A noted failure due to the missing part.
- The model restored to its original state after reinserting the part.

### State Inspection
- Confirm the part is correctly reinserted.
- Verify that the model is stable and structurally sound.

## Challenge Lab: Independent Advanced Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Advanced Model Building
1. **Choose a Complex Model:**
   - Select a more complex LEGO model from your set or an additional set.
   - Follow the instructions to build the model.

2. **Innovate:**
   - Modify the model by adding your own design elements.
   - Ensure the modifications do not compromise the structural integrity.

### Scenario 2: Troubleshooting a Faulty Model
1. **Build a Faulty Model:**
   - Intentionally build a model with errors (e.g., incorrect part placement, missing parts).

2. **Diagnose and Fix:**
   - Identify and fix the errors in the model.
   - Document the process and the solutions applied.

### Expected Output
- A complex LEGO model built with modifications.
- A faulty model diagnosed and fixed.

### State Inspection
- Verify that the modified model is stable and functional.
- Confirm that the faulty model has been corrected and is stable.

---

This lab guide provides a comprehensive approach to building, diagnosing, and troubleshooting LEGO models, emphasizing the importance of precision and structural integrity.
