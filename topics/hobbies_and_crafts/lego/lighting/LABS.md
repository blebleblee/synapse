# Hands-On Operational Lab: Lighting
## Objective & Lab Prerequisites

### Objective
The objective of this lab is to explore, build, and deploy a functional lighting system using LEGO components. This lab will cover the basics of electrical circuits, mechanical assembly, and troubleshooting. By the end of this lab, you will have a working LEGO lighting system and the skills to diagnose and fix common issues.

### Prerequisites
- LEGO Technic set with motors, gears, and lights (e.g., LEGO Power Functions or LEGO Boost)
- LEGO Technic building elements (beams, axles, connectors)
- Basic understanding of electrical circuits and LEGO building techniques
- Access to a computer with LEGO software (LEGO Digital Designer, LEGO Boost App, or similar)

## Exercise 1: Exploration, Diagnostics & System State Inspection

### Step 1: Assemble the Basic Frame
1. **Build a simple frame** using LEGO beams and connectors. Ensure the frame is sturdy and can support the weight of the lighting system.
2. **Attach the motor** to the frame. Ensure the motor is securely mounted and can rotate freely.

### Step 2: Connect the Light
1. **Attach the light** to the motor shaft or another part of the frame. Ensure the light is securely mounted.
2. **Connect the light to the motor controller**. Use the appropriate LEGO cables to connect the light to the motor controller.

### Step 3: Power Up and Inspect
1. **Power up the system** using the LEGO battery pack.
2. **Inspect the system** for proper operation. The light should turn on when the motor is activated.

### Expected Output
- The light turns on when the motor is activated.
- The system is stable and does not wobble or fall apart.

### State Inspection Steps
- Check that all connections are secure.
- Verify that the motor and light are properly connected to the controller.
- Ensure the battery pack is fully charged and properly connected.

## Exercise 2: Building, Configuring & Deploying a Production Scenario

### Step 1: Design the Lighting System

## Exercise 3: Fault Injection, Failure Analysis & Debugging

### Step 1: Introduce a Fault
1. **Introduce a fault** into the system. For example, disconnect a cable, loosen a motor, or remove a light.
2. **Observe the system** for signs of failure. The light may not turn on, the motor may not rotate, or the system may be unstable.

### Step 2: Diagnose the Fault
1. **Diagnose the fault** by systematically checking each component. Start with the power source and work your way through the system.
2. **Identify the root cause** of the fault. Determine whether the issue is with a mechanical component, electrical connection, or software configuration.

### Step 3: Fix the Fault
1. **Fix the fault** by correcting the identified issue. Reconnect cables, tighten motors, or replace faulty components.
2. **Test the system** to ensure the fault has been resolved. The system should function as intended with all lights turning on and mechanical components operating correctly.

### Expected Output
- The fault is identified and corrected.
- The system functions as intended with all lights turning on and mechanical components operating correctly.

### State Inspection Steps
- Check that all connections are secure.
- Verify that all motors and lights are properly connected to the controller.
- Ensure the battery pack is fully charged and properly connected.
