## 1. Underlying Mechanics & Deep Theory

### 1.1 Lego System Mechanics
Lego bricks are composed of interlocking elements that allow for the construction of complex structures. The mechanics of Lego involve understanding the interlocking mechanism, the forces acting on the bricks, and the stability of the structures built.

#### 1.1.1 Interlocking Mechanism
The interlocking mechanism of Lego bricks is based on a stud and tube system. Each brick has studs on the top and tubes on the bottom. The studs fit into the tubes of the brick below, creating a secure connection. The interlocking mechanism is designed to withstand significant forces, but the exact strength depends on the number of bricks and the configuration of the structure.

#### 1.1.2 Forces and Stability
The stability of a Lego structure is influenced by the distribution of forces. The primary forces acting on a Lego structure are gravity, which pulls the structure downward, and the normal force, which is the force exerted by the bricks on each other. The stability of a structure can be analyzed using principles of statics, which involves the study of forces in equilibrium.

### 1.2 Mathematical Models
Mathematical models can be used to analyze the stability and strength of Lego structures. One common model is the truss model, which represents a structure as a network of beams connected at joints. The truss model can be used to analyze the forces acting on each beam and joint in the structure.

#### 1.2.1 Truss Model
The truss model is a simplified representation of a structure that can be used to analyze the forces acting on the structure. The truss model consists of a network of beams connected at joints. Each beam can be modeled as a two-force member, which means that the only forces acting on the beam are the forces at the ends of the beam. The forces at the ends of the beam can be determined using the method of joints, which involves solving a system of equations that represent the equilibrium of each joint in the truss.
